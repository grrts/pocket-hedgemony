# Decisions across contexts

On 17 October 2026 Leroy asked Claude to answer the open questions itself, in the spirit of the gameplay built so far. This page holds the decisions that cross several contexts. Each context's `spec.md` §13 records its own decisions with the reason. Every context follows the cross-context decisions below.

## How the questions were decided

1. **The latest decision wins.** When two designs disagree, the one decided later is followed. For example, trade v2 beats province §7, recruiting v3 beats warfare v5's recruiting text, and screen flow G6 beats warfare §7.15.
2. **Choose the simpler rule.** When a choice adds a mechanic, take the option with fewer moving parts. Never add a mechanic the designs don't already contain.
3. **Keep what the game already does.** Turns last a month and run as a day loop inside it. Everything is deterministic from the seed. Settings never change the simulation. The player acts and the AI follows the same rules.
4. **Missing numbers get a provisional value.** It goes into the context's data JSON with `"provisional": true` beside it or in a `_provisional` list, so balancing can find it.
5. **Not everything is a design question.** Release tasks stay open as `OQ-…` marked *(release task)*: store figures, paid reviewers, device checks, legal or name reviews, listening approval. Code cannot answer them.
6. **Nothing may copy EU4.** A decision never brings in an EU4 rule, name or number.

## Cross-context decisions

| Id | Question (was) | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| X-1 | When a battle stops the turn (OQ-TIME-3, OQ-WAR-2, OQ-SHELL-2, OQ-MOVE-8, OQ-TIME-15) | *Leroy, 17 Oct.* A battle starts on the day the day loop brings two hostile armies together and is fought over time, not resolved at once. **While any battle the player's army or fleet is in is running, time keeps passing but slows down to steps of 4 hours (6 a day)**. Each step fights one round of every running battle and moves every other mover a sixth of a day, so other armies, the player's and anyone else's, can march in and join a battle while it lasts. A battle ends when one side is beaten or retreats, and then the day loop goes back to whole days. The battle result cannot be skipped. The player watches each of their battles and can change its standing orders or order a retreat at any step. When several player battles run at once, the player can switch between them to follow each one's progress and decide when to retreat. **Battles between AI realms never slow the game:** each day they fight their six rounds at once inside the normal day loop, so they last the same time as a player battle, and other armies can still join them at the start of a day. Any battle can be replayed afterwards from its notice. |
| X-2 | The order systems run in a turn (OQ-TIME-2, OQ-TIME-12, OQ-TIME-13, OQ-TIME-14) | **1. Orders:** the player's orders first, then each AI realm's in ascending nation index. **2. Day loop, the 7 days of the turn (X-18):** each day, armies move, then fleets, then traders, then exploration ships, each kind in ascending id. Arrivals come first, then battles (X-1; while a player battle runs the day is split into six 4-hour steps, each moving every mover a sixth of a day and fighting one round of every running battle). Sieges update once a month, in the monthly steps before province. Weekly steps run at the end of every turn (X-18). **3. Monthly steps**, only in a turn in which a new month begins (X-18): sieges, province (people, food, tax), buildings, recruiting, realm (treasury, lords), research, trade monthly upkeep, subjects, government, diplomacy, culture, religion, rebellions, revolutions. **4. Seasonal steps** in the turn in which a season begins (X-4). **5. Yearly steps** in the turn in which 1 January falls. **6. Events**, then the national strengths tick. **7. Achievements**, always last. **8. Autosave.** **9. The date moves on 7 days.** Ties on the same day go by kind order, then ascending id. |
| X-3 | Month length (OQ-TIME-4) | *Leroy, 17 Oct.* The game follows the real calendar: a month has its real length, 28 to 31 days, with leap years under the Gregorian rules applied to every year (no switch from the Julian calendar in 1582, to keep it simple). Things measured per day, such as marching, sailing, traders and battles, use the real number of days. Things measured per month, such as tax, wages, upkeep and growth, stay one amount per month whatever the month's length. Weekly steps (army food and hunger, plunder, raids and plague) run once a turn; see X-18. |
| X-4 | Seasons and harvest (OQ-TIME-5, OQ-TIME-6) | Northern hemisphere: spring is March to May, summer June to August, autumn September to November, winter December to February. The southern hemisphere is the other way round, decided by the province's latitude. The harvest comes in September in the north and March in the south. Yearly steps run in the January turn. |
| X-5 | Start date (OQ-TIME-1, OQ-SETUP-4) | Every campaign, the tutorial included, starts on **11 November 1444**, the day the country catalogue and relations data describe, the day after Varna. The tutorial is still called "The Year of Varna". Loading screens say "Autumn 1444". The engine's `startDate` should move to November 1444 (a request to the game build). |
| X-6 | Trade v2 replaces province §7 (OQ-TRADE-7, OQ-PROV-1, OQ-REALM-1, OQ-BLD-2, OQ-HER-4, OQ-RSCH-4) | Province §7 is dropped: no market price, no trade balance, no yearly trader paths. **Materials:** bought at a fixed base price per good from the rules data, or 25 % dearer when no trade route touches the province. **Grain:** "sell surplus" sells to the realm's own treasury at a fixed grain price, with no market. **Heritage levers:** `trade_balance_pct` and `traders_passing_pct` both become a percentage on *trade ratio gain*; `trader_gain_pct` is a percentage on each deposit; `trader_count` gives extra starting traders. **Research:** the T-branch takes trade v2's effects, and T8 and T9 also keep exploration's +1 colonial governor slot. The realm card shows Traders and Trade ships instead of Merchants. |
| X-7 | Recruiting v3 (OQ-RECR-4/5/6, OQ-WAR-6, OQ-PROV-5) | Recruiting v3 is followed everywhere. A levy takes every class by its share, and there is no "homeless first". The same holds for a sailor muster: there is no "seafolk first". Class recruit ceilings, *Fallen from here*, *Veterans here*, the **Marshal** official and the **deputy** all exist. When a levy goes home the arms always go back to the store; the player makes no choice (Leroy, 17 Oct). Commanders can be the ruler or an heir, a lord (who leads only his own retinue's army), a hired captain, one risen from the ranks, an academy graduate, an exile, or a commissioned official, with the catches warfare §4.4 gives each. A new commander's style follows WAR-139, and a commissioned official counts as an academy graduate. |
| X-8 | Subject kinds (OQ-SUBJ-1/2/3, OQ-DIPL-10, OQ-DIPL-33) | The subjects design's six kinds are the only kinds. The eight kinds in relation-rules are mapped onto them in the data. Whether a subject joins wars and runs its own diplomacy follows the subjects design: diplomacy sends the call, and subjects answers it by the subject's loyalty. Estates are not subjects: they become the realm's lords. Serbia in the declaration example is a tributary that does not join. |
| X-9 | How rebels form (OQ-REBL-1, OQ-PROV-2, OQ-REVO-3) | The rebellions design governs. At 4 or more grievances a province is *Plotting*, with a monthly chance to rise of 5 %, 15 % or 30 % at 4, 5 or 6+ grievances, and a spark can start a rising at 3. When a rising starts, its men come from the province's recruit pool. "A share of the recruits each month" is dropped. A province whose castle a revolution camp holds counts as being at the Rising tier. |
| X-10 | Funding rebels and revolutions (OQ-REVO-1, OQ-DIPL-23) | There is one action, *Fund rebels*, owned by diplomacy, and it uses revolutions §7.1's numbers, since it was decided later the same day. The chance of being caught is 3 % a month. Being caught gives the target the caught war reason. Diplomacy's *Foreign agitators* and *Foreign meddling* grievances are dropped in favour of this one action. The province-level gold choices and the per-official catch chances are dropped too: the catch chance is a flat 3 %. |
| X-11 | Truce length (OQ-DIPL-3) | Diplomacy §8 governs: a truce lasts 5 years, plus 5 more if land changed hands or a subject was made. A white peace gives 5 years. The relations data and the engine's 60 months (5 years) already match the base. |
| X-12 | How a campaign is won (OQ-SHELL-1, OQ-REALM-6, OQ-TIME-10, OQ-ACH-14) | There are two ways to win. **World domination:** owning or holding as subjects half of the world's settled land provinces (land provinces with people; allies, by marriage or otherwise, don't count). It is checked in every January turn, and reaching it wins at once. **Best at the end:** on 31 August 1939 the campaign ends, and a realm that leads the Economy, Power or Technology score wins in that score. Scores are worked out each January for the realm book, and the winner is judged only at the end. No margin is needed, and ties share the win. After a domination win the player may keep playing to the end date. A campaign that reaches the end without a win shows the final scores and the player's rank. The "lead by 50 % for 25 years" placeholder is dropped. Realm §12 owns the score formulas. |
| X-13 | The catalogue against the map (OQ-SETUP-1, OQ-SETUP-2) | The map's nations are the realms on the map and the playable list. A catalogue record that owns no map province is reference data that later events can draw on, not a realm. The 37 ids that differ are matched through a rename table in the scenario-setup data. Only a representation that owns provinces becomes a realm. |
| X-14 | Content after 1939 (OQ-EVT-1, OQ-TIME-8, OQ-RSCH-1, OQ-WAR-12, OQ-TIME-9) | Nothing in play happens after 31 August 1939. Events dated after it are dropped. An age or period whose window opens before the end date is cut at the end date, and an age that would start on a post-1939 event never starts. In Era VII, every invention known by 1939 stays: radio, antibiotics, assembly line, motor and air transport, plastics, highways, airfields, tractors, hybrid seeds, aircraft carriers, radar, tanks, military aircraft, field radio, planned economy and welfare state. Their effects are trimmed to what they did by 1939. **Nuclear physics and rocketry are dropped.** Fighting styles first seen after the end date are dropped. Eras follow the calendar. |
| X-15 | Settings that might change the simulation (OQ-SET-1, OQ-VIEW-9) | Settings never change results. Battery saver only lowers the frame rate, animation and music voices. The AI's thinking budget is counted in work steps, not time, and it is the same on every device and setting. `engine.worker_budget` is dropped. |
| X-16 | Save header flags (OQ-SAVE-1, OQ-ACH-4, OQ-TUT-6) | The save header gains `ironman_unbroken`, `cheat_marked`, `switched_country` and `began_as_tutorial`. Tutorial progress and Needs attention mutes belong to the player, not the campaign: they live in the player profile and sync with it, as the tutorial design says. Chapter saves of the tutorial are read-only. Lifetime achievement counters and the demo's local list live in the player profile, not in a save. |
| X-17 | Seasons on the map (Leroy, 17 Oct) | **How it looks.** The map follows the season of each province's hemisphere (X-4). In winter, snow covers land from latitude 50° toward the pole and every mountain province. In spring, green comes back from the south, in the north. Summer is the base palette. In autumn, forests and fields turn gold. Dry lands show their wet season. These are palette swaps on the pixel tiles, chosen per province each month, so they cost nothing per frame. **What it does.** The seasonal rules already in the specs show on the map: snow passes close (MOVE-030), fords open in summer and autumn, floods last 3 months, wet seasons hit the tropics, and harvests come in September in the north and March in the south. Two map effects are new and kept small. First, **frozen seas:** from December to March, the northern sea zones that froze historically (the Gulf of Bothnia, the White Sea and the far north) close to fleets, and their ports cannot launch or take in ships. Second, **frozen rivers:** in the same months, a river at latitude 55° or more freezes and is crossed like a bridge, with no river penalty. The lists of frozen zones and the latitude lines are provisional data in world-map. Settings cannot turn the seasons off; colour-vision modes keep the season palettes readable. |
| X-18 | Turn length (Leroy, 17 Oct) | **A turn is one week: 7 days.** The first turn starts on 11 November 1444 (X-5), and the last ends on 31 August 1939, which may make it shorter than 7 days. Each turn the player gives orders, then the day loop plays the 7 days (with 4-hour steps while a player battle runs, X-1). Weekly steps run at the end of every turn. **Monthly, seasonal and yearly rules keep their size and their rhythm:** they run once, in the turn in which a new month, season or year begins, at the end of that turn. So tax, wages, upkeep, growth, research and every other monthly amount stay exactly as specified, and the economy is balanced as before. **No rule counts in turns** (Leroy, 17 Oct): every duration, delay or cadence a spec wrote in turns is rewritten as a span of time (days, weeks, months, seasons or years), chosen for what the rule means, and the engine measures it by the date, not by counting turns. Only things that truly happen once per turn, such as giving orders, the turn report and End turn, stay per turn. A campaign is about 25,800 turns. Autosave and the turn report work per turn; the rotating autosave is kept to monthly so it doesn't write 4 times as often. |
