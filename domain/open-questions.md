- **D-AUD-11**: (was OQ-AUD-11) Palettes: there is no blend; Atlantic and creole nations use the single `atlantic_creole` palette. The turkic split by capital longitude 55 (west `mena`, otherwise `steppe`) stays in config. A `narrative_only` major event plays no stinger, so there is no "sombre" variant. Simplest rules that need no new tracks.
- **D-AUD-12**: (was OQ-AUD-12) Catalogue defaults: `ui_tooltip` is never played (no setting turns it on); all five haptic moments are "light" (§15.3 is later); the `final_result` screen plays `campaign_end`. The later decision and the screen list win.
- **D-AUD-13**: (17 Oct, Leroy: pull music and sound forward for playtesting) The music and effects are built ahead of their milestones in a first cut: the director, 16 written tracks (title, two peace tunes per half of the eras, prosperity, diplomacy, the five war and situation tracks with layers, six stingers) and 30 effects, synthesized in the page (AUD-141). The worklet, the MML build, ambience, captions, haptics and the full catalogue follow in their milestones; the palette tunings are drafts awaiting review (OQ-AUD-13).
- **D-AUD-14**: (17 Oct, Leroy: "its not really nice to listen to, it sounds like noise mostly"; the effects are fine) The music leaves the strict PH-5 chip rules of AUD-004 that made it harsh: square and narrow pulses buzzed, the 16-step envelopes and stepped triangle crackled, and the noise voice ticked on most beats. The music keeps the voice roles (P1, P2, T, W, N), the tunes, the motif and the palettes, and plays them soft (AUD-143), with a pad voice holding chords and a small hall where AUD-004 and §2 forbid reverb tails. Measured on the first tracks, spectral flatness (how much of the sound is noise) falls from about 0.36 to under 0.08 and the brightness centre from about 3 kHz to about 1 kHz. The effects keep the chip sound unchanged. The worklet synth (AUD-104) follows this decision when it is built.
- **D-AUD-15**: (18 Oct, feedback 79, second pass) The soft voices fixed the sound but not the writing: tunes ignored their chords (in the early peace tune about a fifth of the time two voices rubbed a semitone or tritone apart, up to 46 % in major palettes, where a chord on degree 7 is diminished), the bass's "fifth" was a tritone on some roots, narrow 12.5 % pulses buzzed, and `b6` and `b7` in minor tunes sounded the wrong notes. The tunes are rewritten harmony first (AUD-144) with the motif kept: the title gets eight bars, the early peace tune a pastoral and a dance, the late one a march, and the war, siege and crisis tracks follow their chords. Clashes fall to about 5 % of the time (passing notes) in every palette, and the lead sits on its chord more than 95 % of the time.
- **D-AUD-16**: (19 Oct, Leroy chose "Real instruments" after the play-hook review, `research/play-hook.md`) The music, reworked twice on chip voices (D-AUD-14, D-AUD-15), still sounded like noise, and EU4's hold comes partly from a score on real instruments. Our own tunes, the motif and the music director stay, but every music voice plays on sampled period instruments (AUD-145), one band per palette: Western Europe recorder, flute and lute (nylon guitar), Eastern Europe fiddle and dulcimer, the Middle East oboe and qanun (koto) with the shawm in war, the Steppe fiddle and dombra (shamisen), South Asia flute and sitar, East Asia shakuhachi and koto, Southeast Asia flute and gambang (marimba), West Africa kora (harp) and balafon (marimba), East and Southern Africa flute and mbira (kalimba), the Americas pan flute and ocarina, Oceania flute and marimba, the Atlantic fiddle and banjo; cello and a string section under all of them, a choir under the title, trumpets, horns or shawms leading in war. The notes come from the Fluid R3 GM soundfont (Frank Wen, CC BY 3.0, credited by AUD-147), cut by `tools/music-samples` into 27 packs of 2 to 15 notes (3.6 MB in all, about 1 MB a palette). It overturns "no samples, no audio files" for the music only (AUD-003, AUD-008); the effects keep the chip (AUD-002).
- **D-AUD-17**: (19 Oct, Leroy chose "Real instruments", which put the map ambience on by default with it) EU4's map is never silent. The ambience bed goes on by default (AUD-080) and plays under the music, about 10 dB down, and at full level in the silences between tracks (AUD-082, AUD-093), where before it was off and heard only in the silences. The beds stay synthesized (AUD-148). A device that saved the old default gets ambience on once.

### Culture (culture)

- **D-CULT-1**: (was OQ-CULT-1) Capacity weight: dropped. A province's language adds no weight to the officials or lords caps; a foreign-language province costs only through its officials' 10-year learning time (CULT-050). Government v2, the later design, counts officials and lords, not provinces.
- **D-CULT-2**: (was OQ-CULT-2) Nation state and form limits: no government form limits the Language policy (CULT-093). A "nation state" for merging without an event is a country whose court language is the `merges_into` of some language (Italian, German); after 50 years of *Impose* it merges, in its provinces, the languages whose `merges_into` is its court language. Government v2 has no such limits and no nation-state form, and the language data already names the national languages.
- **D-CULT-3**: (was OQ-CULT-3) Family count: 43 families, the event tree's 39 `culture_groups` plus celtic, baltic, caucasian and hebrew; English and Dutch get the family germanic in the data. The data is the count; "38 + 4 = 42" was a miscount.
- **D-CULT-4**: (was OQ-CULT-4) Nationalism for drift: the province's own nationalism support (the event tree's per-province value) is read; at 0.3 or more every drift in the province is multiplied by 0.25. The event tree stores nationalism per province, not per language.
- **D-CULT-5**: (was OQ-CULT-5) Fourth language: one rule for languages and faiths. A newcomer enters only if larger than the smallest of the three; the smallest group of the four (the old smallest, or the newcomers) joins the majority. One storage rule should have one behaviour, and nobody is lost.
- **D-CULT-6**: (was OQ-CULT-6) Changes for other designs: culture's rules are followed where the older province and country stats text differs: Language % counts related languages half, *Foreign rule* is for homelands of foreign languages (related ones from nationalism 0.3), officials learn by relation, settlers may go to any own province with free tiles, and the four new event verbs and five triggers exist. The music palettes for the new families follow the culture proposal (celtic 1, baltic 2, caucasian 2, hebrew 3) for audio to take. Culture's design is the later one on these points.
- **D-CULT-7**: (was OQ-CULT-7) Lords waver under *Impose*: once, when the policy turns to *Impose*, foreign-language lords first; keeping it costs nothing more. The simpler rule, and the yearly cost is already the *Banned language* grievance.
- **D-CULT-8**: (was OQ-CULT-8) Lords' language and loyalty: a same-language lord (or one of a house the ruler married into) has the realm's rising cause "sharing your language"; a foreign-language lord starts one step lower; a related-language lord has neither. Country stats §5.5 already lists sharing your language as a loyalty cause.
- **D-CULT-9**: (was OQ-CULT-9) Own kind of land: steppe is grassland or drylands ground, deep forest is forest or jungle ground, islands is any province touching the sea (CULT-092). These are the world map's own grounds and coasts; no new terrain is needed.
- **D-CULT-10**: (was OQ-CULT-10) New language abroad: the region is a map region; it is separated by sea when its top-level map region (continent) differs from that of the court's capital. Only languages with a `splits_to` entry split, and the split language and the old court count each other as related. The map's region tree already exists.
- **D-CULT-11**: (was OQ-CULT-11) Local official: while an official of the province's majority language serves in a foreign-language province, *Banned language* is not given there; *Foreign rule* stays. Grievances are counts, so "not made worse" means one grievance fewer.
- **D-CULT-12**: (was OQ-CULT-12) *Gather our kin*: diplomacy owns it as a war aim, like *Free a people*; culture supplies the condition. War aims live in diplomacy.
- **D-CULT-13**: (was OQ-CULT-13) Missing keys: `risings.homeland_draw` 0.08, `refugees.settle_after_years` 2 and `engine_events.check_every_years` 5 (counted from 1444) are in `culture.json` and listed as provisional. The design says every threshold lives in config.
- **D-CULT-14**: (was OQ-CULT-14) Trading people and Language %: confirmed weight 0; they are neither the same language nor foreign for grievances. They are not of the court's language, and the design only shields them from grievances.
- **D-CULT-15**: (was OQ-CULT-15) Schools of a learned language: a school teaching Latin or Classical Arabic has no teaching language and speeds no drift; other schools carry a language id. Nobody's home language is Latin, so such a school can never be in the pull language.
- **D-CULT-16**: (was OQ-CULT-16) Released country: it takes the catalogue record whose first culture is the language (X-13 keeps such records as reference data); if none fits, a generated country named after the language, with that language as court language and the form of the country it left. The fewest new parts.
- **D-CULT-18**: First game slice (provisional): each map realm's 1444 languages come from its catalogue culture strings, mapped once onto about 190 languages by hand (merchant colonies, captives and peoples the catalogue says live in a separate record left out), up to three per realm, every province of the realm taking the realm's mix; per-province homelands wait on the homeland data. A province's homeland is its 1444 majority language. Lords and officials carry no language yet, so a lord counts as of the court language and any official governs for the court. A realm's own 1444 land counts as held long enough for the court's pull and is never under *Foreign rule*, as the game already did ("the people were not yours in 1444"): a court that ruled them for generations is not foreign there, and nobody starts with the grievance. Without this, 394 provinces of thirteen realms (the Timurids, Lithuania, the Mamluks …) would start under *Foreign rule*.

### Tribes (tribes)

- **D-TRIBE-1**: Which tribes: "Indian tribes" is read as the peoples of North America, where the five tribal realms of the 1444 map sit. The Caribbean, South America and Africa are left to their own designs; `tribes.regions` and `tribes.new_tribe_regions` let them join by data without a new rule.
- **D-TRIBE-2**: New tribes (Leroy, 18 Oct, "introduce more tribes that historically existed, even really small"): every `decentralized_polity` record of `am_north` that existed on 11 November 1444 and owns no province becomes a realm. Susquehannock (a people that formed c. 1450) and Paquimé (`background_only`) are left out.
- **D-TRIBE-3**: Where they go: each new tribe gets a home point (longitude and latitude of its heartland from its catalogue record) in the scenario data, like the map's capitals. A home province already owned in 1444 (Coosa under the Creek, perhaps Hopi and Zuni under the Pueblo) leaves the record as reference data (TRIBE-004), the simplest rule and the one X-13 already follows.
- **D-TRIBE-4**: Keep gameplay as it is (Leroy, 18 Oct): no new resource, no new screen besides one tab, no new war rule. Hunting grounds reuse wrongs, the war aim *Punish*, the reasons count, foraging, self-run provinces and the officials cap. A tribe grows by claiming and taking in at about one province every few years per place in its range, slower than conquest and far slower than a colonial power, but it is never boxed in.
- **D-TRIBE-5**: Pueblo: its 1444 form is a city commune (town polities), so it is not a tribal realm and gets no range; the Rio Grande towns grew by farming, not by walking new land. A reform into a tribal form would give it one.
- **D-TRIBE-7**: Built 18 October 2026. Tribal regions are read off the map as a box of longitude and latitude per region (`tribes.region_bounds`), since map realms carry no catalogue region; North America is west of 50° W and north of 24.5° N. The asking price and the reasons count's numbers are in `tribes.ask`. "Needs no official" (GOV-023) now holds in a campaign: a clan council or chiefdom's own-people province with no official is not *Ungoverned*.
- **D-TRIBE-6**: Hunting grounds stay unowned, so the colony rules (D-EXPL-2, PROV-250) are unchanged: anyone may still settle there, at the price of a fiercer people and an angry tribe, or by asking first.
