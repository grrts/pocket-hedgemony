# Subjects

Subjects are countries on the map that answer to another country, their overlord. This system decides what kinds of subject exist, what each side owes the other, how loyal a subject is and why, and how a subject is made, tightened, loosened, folded into your realm, released or lost. Loyalty is never a hidden number: it is one of four steps, moved each season by named reasons you can read on the subject's card.

## How it works

### The ladder of rule

All land sits on one **ladder of rule**: royal land (run by your officials), lord's land (run by a named lord inside your realm), subject realm, and independent. The one rule that separates a lord from a subject: a **subject** has its own country on the map with its own treasury, army, lords and diplomacy, while a **lord** is a card inside your realm. At the start, any catalogue subject that is not its own map nation becomes a lord of its overlord, his card filled from the catalogue's history.

### Six kinds of subject

| | Vassal | Tributary | Union | March | Colony | Protectorate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Icon | shield | chest | two crowns | watchtower | anchor | key |
| Pays you | 10 % of its taxes | 15 % of its income, in gold or grain | 5 % | nothing | 25 %, and its trade goods flow home first | 10 % |
| Fights for you | when called, by loyalty, like a lord | never | always, if Loyal or Wavering | only in wars on its own frontier; raids your enemies there | defends itself only | when called |
| Own diplomacy | yes, but no wars or alliances against you or your allies | yes, fully | no: your envoys speak for it | yes, but no alliances | no | no |
| Own subjects | yes | yes | yes | no | no | no |
| You owe it | defence when attacked | defence against anyone but you | defence; one ruler for both | a yearly stipend of gold or titles | defence; ships on its sea route | defence |
| Reach weight | 1 | ½ | 1 | ½ | 1 | 1 |
| Drift | none | −1 | none | −1 | none | −1 |
| Fold in | yes | no: tighten to Vassal first | yes, as *Merge the crowns* | yes | yes, into royal land | yes |
| 1444 examples | Brittany under France; shugo houses under the Ashikaga; Romagna lords under the Pope | Joseon, Ryukyu, Dai Viet to the Ming; Serbia, Byzantium to the Ottomans; Granada to Castile | Sweden, Norway under Denmark; Sicily, Sardinia under Aragon | Jurchen and Tusi commanderies on the Ming frontier | Genoese Chios and Gazaria; Portuguese Ceuta | Bukhara under Russia; Indian princely states under the Company (event tree) |

A subject pays its share from its own treasury each month, so a poor subject pays little. Tribute taken in grain arrives at your nearest castle and counts toward Food. Protectorates exist only from era VI, or from era IV for an overseas trade empire.

**Ties** are labels on the bond that change one or two rules:

| Tie | Set when | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Kin | its ruler is of your ruling house (appanages) | +1 loyalty reason; if your line dies out, its ruler has a claim to your throne |
| Held of the Church | the overlord is a religious head (the Pope's fiefs) | pays a token (1 %), never fights |
| Held in fief | a sovereign kingdom holds its crown from someone else | pays a token (1 %), never called to war; counts ½ for reach |
| Second master | it also pays tribute to someone else | the second master gets a small tribute (5 %) and no other rights; if the two masters fight, it leans to the stronger |
| Hostage at court | you hold its heir | +1 reason; −2 if he is mistreated or lost |

**Colony stages:**

| Stage | When | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Outpost | founded | pays 25 %, no army of its own, your governor (an official) runs it |
| Settled | 5+ provinces, more than half settler people | raises its own militia; its settlers become a peace voice that can be Wavering; from here its loyalty is counted each season (an Outpost stays Loyal) |
| Self-governing | 10+ provinces and 50 years, or an event (dominion) | pays 10 %, keeps its own lords; can no longer be folded in, only released or kept |

When a Settled or Self-governing colony turns Disloyal, [Revolutions](Revolutions) gets the settler-independence trigger.

### The 1444 subjects

The relations data has eight subject kinds, which are mapped onto the six: vassal to Vassal; fief to Vassal with *Held of the Church* or *Held in fief* (Naples from the Pope, Andorra from Foix); appanage to Vassal with *Kin*; tributary to Tributary; personal union to Union; frontier commandery to March; colony to Colony; and estate (Catalonia, Flanders, the Castilian magnates, Moravia and others) to a lord of its crown, never a separate country. Event subjects map the same way, with protectorate and puppet becoming Protectorate and dominion a Colony at Self-governing. The 167 Subject records minus 32 estates give **135 subjects** in 1444: 83 vassals, 31 tributaries, 9 unions, 7 marches and 5 colonies.

### Loyalty

A subject has one of the lords' four steps:

| Step | Pays | When called to war | Foreign envoys | Other |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Loyal | all it owes | comes at once, whole army | turns them away | can be folded in |
| Wavering | half | half its army, late; home after 6 months unless the war is on its border | listens | |
| Disloyal | nothing | refuses; each further call is another down reason | bargains for a backer | hides its army's size |
| In revolt | nothing | at war with you: the independence war | | |

**Reasons** are named badges pointing up or down, each worth one or more arrows. At the end of every season (3 months) the arrows are added: +2 or more moves loyalty one step up, −2 or less one step down, anything else stays. Loyalty never moves twice in the same direction within 12 months.

| Up | Arrows | Down | Arrows |
|---|---|---|---|
| You are much stronger (your soldiers ≥ 3 × theirs) | +1 | You are not much stronger (theirs ≥ ½ of yours) | −1 |
| Same faith as your ruler | +1 | Different faith | −1 (−2 if you persecute it) |
| Same people as your ruler | +1 | Different people, not accepted | −1 |
| You defended it in the last 20 years | +1 | You left it alone (attacked, you didn't join), 20 years | −1 |
| Gift in the last 5 years | +1 | Heavy / crushing tribute | −1 / −2 |
| Marriage between your houses | +1 | You lost a war in the last 10 years | −1 |
| Kin; Hostage at court (each, so both is +2) | +1 | New ruler on your throne (first 5 years) | −1 |
| Your resident at its court | +1 | Courted (a foreign envoy stationed at its court) | −1 |
| Common enemy (the same rival) | +1 | Stretched thin (over reach) | −1; −2 for the furthest few |
| Long held (30+ years) | +1 | Far away (no land border, and no sea route: both of you holding coast on a linked sea) | −1 |
| Light tribute | +1 | Outgrown (more provinces than you, or than its kind allows: a Vassal over half of yours) | −1 |
| Given a crown (raised while Wavering), 10 years | +1 | Master of others | −3 |
| Loosened, 10 years | +2 | Hostile / Rival stance toward you | −2 / −1 |
| | | Kind's drift | −1 |
| | | Force used (you folded in, deposed or restored another subject by force), 5 years | −1 |
| | | Another subject deposed, 5 years | −1 |
| | | Doctrine of lapse used on another, 10 years | −1 |
| | | Refused a call again, 12 months | −1 |
| | | Missed march stipend, 12 months | −1 |
| | | You left another subject alone, 5 years | −1 |
| | | Two quarrelling subjects forbidden, 5 years | −1 |

Events can add or remove a reason by id, or add a one-off named reason with its own arrow for a stated time.

**Starting loyalty in 1444** comes from the dataset's autonomy: very low, low and moderate start Loyal; high and very high start Wavering; one step lower if the subject is Hostile toward its overlord in the data.

### What an overlord can do

Each action is a button on the subject's card; a locked button names its first missing requirement.

| Action | Cost | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Set tribute (none, light, normal, heavy, crushing) | none | pays ×0, ×0.5, ×1, ×1.5, ×2 of the kind's share; light +1, heavy −1, crushing −2 |
| Take tribute in grain | none | arrives as grain at your nearest castle |
| Call to war | none | answered like a lord, by loyalty and kind; tributaries are never called |
| Send a gift | 6 months of its income | +1 for 5 years |
| Station a resident | one envoy while it stays | +1; reveals a courting foreign envoy; shows its true army even when Disloyal |
| Lend an official | one official's wages | +1; the official's type effect applies there |
| Take a hostage | only from a Loyal or Wavering subject, or as a peace term | *Hostage at court*; the heir returns when its ruler dies; harming him −2 |
| Arrange a marriage | an heir or princess | the marriage reason; a path to a Union |
| Tighten (Tributary to Vassal only) | one envoy for 6 months; subject Loyal | a Wavering subject refuses and drops a step; Vassal to Union is not possible |
| Loosen (Vassal or Protectorate to Tributary) | none | +2 for 10 years |
| Fold in | see below | it becomes one of your lords |
| Release | none | independent and Friendly toward you; no other subject's reasons change |
| Depose its ruler | a pretender of yours, or its ruler Disloyal (if In revolt, win first) | your candidate rules, starting Loyal; every other subject −1 for 5 years |

**Raising a lord.** A Loyal or Wavering lord holding at least 3 provinces can be raised to a Vassal, or to a March if his land borders a foreign realm (you choose): his provinces become his own country on the map and his retinue its army. A Wavering lord raised gets *Given a crown* +1 for 10 years; a Loyal one gets nothing extra. He leaves your lord capacity and counts against your subject reach; his land leaves your realm, you get a share instead of half his taxes, and he may make his own friends abroad.

### Obligations both ways

- When anyone but you attacks a subject you owe defence, you are called as if you were its ally. Refusing gives you an Oath broken, the subject *You left it alone*, and every other subject −1 for 5 years.
- Each year a March's stipend is missed, it gets −1.
- A Union shares your ruler, so every war of that ruler is the Union's war, and its lords' peace voices add to yours.
- Two subjects of one overlord can't fight without leave. You pick a side (the loser drops a step) or forbid it (both −1 for 5 years).

### Subject reach

**Subject reach** is how many subjects your ruler can watch over. It comes from [Government](Government), starting from the catalogue's administrative capacity: very low 2, low 3, moderate 5, high 7, very high 9. It is a separate number from lord capacity. Each direct subject counts by its weight (Tributary, March and Held in fief ½, the rest 1). Over reach, every subject gets *Stretched thin* −1, and for each point of reach you are over, one more of the furthest subjects takes −1 more (11 of 7 hits the four furthest).

**Subjects with subjects.** A subject that holds subjects counts 3 times its kind's weight plus 1 for each of its own subjects, and gets *Master of others* −3, which goes away when it releases them. Only the direct overlord acts on a subject; a subject's own subjects pay you nothing and count nothing in your reach. When a subject is folded in or falls, its subjects step up to you, keeping their loyalty. March, Colony and Protectorate can't hold subjects.

### Folding in

Folding in makes a subject's ruler one of your lords and removes its country from the map. It needs: Loyal for the last 10 years, a subject for 20 years or more, a land path or your sea route to it, room for one more lord in lord capacity, and a kind other than Tributary or Self-governing colony. In 1444 each subject counts as held since its dataset start date (20 years when unknown), and one Loyal in 1444 counts as Loyal for 10 years.

You keep one envoy at its court for 1 month per province; if the subject drops to Wavering, the folding stops and the envoy comes home. When it completes, its ruler becomes one lord holding all its provinces (its own lords merged into his card), with its buildings, its army as his retinue and its treasury as his wealth. Its provinces get *Foreign rule* for 10 years unless it shared your faith and people.

- An Outpost or Settled colony folded in becomes royal land at once, needing your officials.
- **Merge the crowns** folds in a Union after 50 years in union with the junior crown Loyal: its provinces join the realm, its lords become yours one by one keeping their loyalty, and its laws become a charter in its provinces.
- When the ruler of a Loyal Vassal or Union dies without an heir, you can inherit and fold it in at once, ignoring the 10 and 20 years. For other subjects its lords pick a new ruler, and you may back a candidate with an envoy.
- With the event flag *doctrine of lapse*, any subject whose ruler dies without a natural heir is folded in, and every other subject takes −1 for 10 years.

### Losing subjects

- **Drifting.** A Disloyal subject stops paying, refuses calls, and bargains with foreign envoys for a **backer** that agrees to declare *Break a subject* when it rises.
- **Rising.** A Disloyal subject rises to In revolt when it has a backer at peace and willing, or its overlord is at war elsewhere with over half its peace voices for peace, or an event says so; the seasonal count alone never goes past Disloyal. Every other Disloyal subject of yours that borders it or shares its backer rises too. The **independence war** starts with its aim fixed as *Independence* and its backer joining. If you win, the peace can restore it one kind tighter, or fold it in at once if it shares a border. If it wins, it goes free with a Hostile stance toward you.
- **Changing masters.** A Disloyal subject bordering a rival of yours can ask to pass to that rival; the rival takes it when it fits within the rival's reach. It becomes the rival's subject of the same kind, and you and the rival turn Hostile toward each other.
- **Taken by force.** *Break a subject* is a war declared on you, the overlord; the subject answers by its kind and loyalty like any call. If the attacker wins, it chooses whether the subject goes free or becomes its own.
- **The overlord's fall.** If an overlord is destroyed or becomes someone's subject, its Tributaries and Marches go free, and its Vassals, Unions and Colonies go to whoever took its capital if it wants them, otherwise free.

### Making a subject

- A peace term *Become a subject* (over three quarters peace voices): the winner picks Vassal or Tributary, or Protectorate from era VI (era IV for an overseas trade empire). A distant loser with no land path can only be a Tributary.
- After a 20-year tribute term has run, the receiver can offer the payer Tributary by envoy, which a weaker payer accepts.
- Winning a war to seat a pretender: the realm becomes your Vassal or ally, the new ruler starting Loyal.
- By agreement through [Diplomacy](Diplomacy): when its count says yes to *Offer overlordship* or *Ask for protection*, the smaller country becomes a Tributary, or a Vassal if it asked for that.
- A ruler inheriting another crown after a marriage makes a Union.
- A **frontier grant**: give your titles and a gift to a frontier chief who is nobody's subject and shares your border, and he becomes a March.
- Chartering a group of your colonial provinces in one distant region makes one Colony.
- Events (`vassalize`, `create_country … subject_of`, `relation: personal_union`) create the kind they name; a created country is a Colony at Self-governing unless named otherwise.

### France, 1444 to 1453

France has high administrative capacity and subject reach 7, with 10 vassals and the appanage of the Dauphiné.

- **The start.** Brittany starts Wavering and Armagnac Loyal. France uses 11 reach of 7, so every subject, Brittany and Foix included, has *Stretched thin*, and the four furthest take −1 more.
- **Folding in the Dauphiné.** The Dauphiné is a Loyal Vassal with *Kin*, a subject since 1349, with 3 provinces. France sends one envoy for 3 months; the Dauphin becomes a great lord inside France, which now uses 10 reach of 7.
- **Two small vassals.** Folding in the French minor lordships and Nevers over the next years brings reach to 8 of 7, and lord capacity fills up instead.
- **Brittany is courted.** England stations an envoy at Brittany's court; France stations a resident there and arranges a marriage. The season ends at 3 up and 3 down, so Brittany stays Wavering. When war with England resumes in 1449 and France calls, Brittany comes with half its army, late.
- **Armagnac.** France sets Armagnac's tribute to heavy to pay for the war. Armagnac turns Disloyal, and an event makes it rise. France wins, the peace folds Armagnac in at once because it shares a border, and every other subject takes *Force used* −1 for 5 years.

### Playing as a subject

Every playable subject in the catalogue (218) can be played from the start. You see your own card from below with the same badges, and can pay and be protected, stop paying, find a backer, wait for your overlord's bad war, or marry into its house to inherit its crown (a union can run either way). A Loyal subject that politely asks by envoy for a looser kind gets it if the overlord's AI is cautious or over reach. A subject that wins its independence war can make its old overlord its own subject in the same peace if the voices allow.

### The computer players

As overlord, an AI folds in Loyal subjects when lord capacity allows and its personality likes centralising, loosens a Disloyal subject when at war elsewhere, stations residents on its biggest Wavering subjects, and never lets reach overflow by more than 1. As a subject, a Disloyal AI looks for a backer; an opportunist rises the moment its overlord is losing a war, a cautious one waits for a backer and its neighbours. As a rival, it courts its rivals' Wavering subjects and picks *Break a subject* against the overlord with the most Disloyal subjects. Loyalty is worked out once a season per subject from cached reasons.

### Each month

A turn is one week; these steps run once a month, at the end of the turn in which the new month begins. Each month tribute is paid, tighten and fold-in envoys progress (stopping if the subject wavers), and risings are checked. At each season's end reasons are recomputed and loyalty moves. Each year march stipends fall due and colony stages are checked.

## What you see

- **The subjects list** (a tab on the country card): reach used of reach, monthly tribute in gold and grain, and one row per subject with its kind icon, loyalty, up and down arrow counts, what it pays and its worst reason, sorted worst first.
- **The subject card**, in the lord card's frame: kind, ruler, provinces, army, treasury, its own subjects, loyalty with its reasons, what it pays and how it comes when called, and the action buttons.
- **World zoom**: a subject in its direct overlord's colour with a 2-pixel diagonal hatch in its own colour and a dotted border with the overlord, never two hatches stacked.
- **Region zoom**: the kind's icon on the subject's capital, its frame tinted by loyalty (green, yellow, orange, red).
- **The subjects lens**: your subjects coloured by loyalty, with a line to each courted subject from the courting country's capital.
- Long-press a subject on the map (or select it and press the card button on a controller) to open its card.

### Subjects in the game today

- **Where**: your realm's **Diplomacy** tab lists your *Subjects*: how much of your reach they use (from your administrative capacity: 2 to 9, 5 where it is unknown), the tribute they send each month, and a row per subject with its kind, loyalty, arrows up and down, what it pays and its worst reason. Tap a subject to open its card.
- **The subject's card** (its own Diplomacy tab): its loyalty and every reason behind it as a badge, what it pays and how it comes to your wars, and your actions: set the tribute (*None*, *Light*, *Normal*, *Heavy*, *Crushing*), *Send a gift* (six months of its income, +1 for 5 years), *Loosen to tributary* (Vassal or Protectorate, +2 for 10 years) and *Release* (it goes free as your friend).
- **What they pay**: the kind's share times the tribute level, all of it while Loyal, half while Wavering and nothing while Disloyal. A Vassal pays 10 in 100 of its taxes; a Tributary 15 in 100 of its income. It shows as *Tribute* in your Treasury tab.
- **Loyalty** is counted at the end of every season: two more arrows up than down raise it a step, two more down lower it, never twice the same way within a year. You hear when it moves. A Disloyal subject refuses your calls to arms.
- **Making subjects**: send an envoy to *Offer overlordship* to a smaller realm (it becomes your tributary) or *Ask for protection* of a larger one (you become its vassal), or demand tribute. The other realms offer overlordship too.
- **Your envoys at their court**: *Station a resident* (+1 loyalty, takes one of your envoys while he stays), *Tighten to vassal* (a Loyal tributary, one envoy for 6 months) and *Fold into your realm* (Loyal for 10 years, a subject for 20, sharing a border, and room under your lords cap; one envoy for a month per province). When folding in completes, their land is yours, held by their ruler as one of your lords, and the country leaves the map. Other realms fold in their subjects too, one at a time.
- **Rising**: a Disloyal subject may rise and declare its independence war. The other realms' subjects rise when their overlord is losing a war elsewhere or a stronger enemy of it waits next door, and their Disloyal neighbours rise with them. Lose land in the peace and the subject is taken back one kind tighter (its fellow subjects remember the force used); otherwise it is free. If you are a subject and Disloyal, *Rise for independence* is on your Diplomacy tab.
- **Their heir**: *Take a hostage* from a Loyal or Wavering subject: his heir lives at your court (+1 loyalty) until you *Send the heir home* or 20 years pass.
- **Backers**: a Disloyal subject with a stronger enemy of its overlord next door rises with that realm at its side; the backer declares war on the overlord at once.
- **The Subjects lens**: from the map lens menu, your realm in its colour, your subjects green, amber or red by loyalty, your overlord purple. Tap one for its Diplomacy tab.
- **Not yet**: merging crowns, courting other realms' subjects and harming a hostage come later; distance gives no reason yet. Faith and tongue do: the same state faith or court tongue pulls a subject closer, a different faith pushes it away, more so where you persecute it.
