# Tutorial and help

Pocket Hegemony teaches itself in four layers: a short tutorial campaign, "The Year of Varna", played as the Ottomans from 11 November 1444, the day after the battle of Varna, to 1447; guided first turns for your first real campaign; a Needs attention list that tells you what needs you; and tooltips, "Why?" and the Almanac, which explain every number in the game. You can skip any of it, and nothing in the game is locked behind it.

## How it works

### The principles

- **Teach by doing.** Every lesson is something you do on the real map with the real screens. No slideshow, and no tutorial-only screens the real game does not have, apart from the tutorial's own pacing tools and its note.
- **One idea per step.** Each step names one thing to look at or one thing to do, in one or two short lines.
- **Short sessions.** Chapters last about 5 minutes. Close the app mid-chapter and you come back to the same step.
- **Never trap.** Every step and every chapter can be skipped, and you can leave the tutorial at any time.
- **Same lesson on every device.** Each step is written once, with button tokens that become the right prompt for touch, mouse and keyboard, or controller.
- **Teach 1444 first.** The tutorial teaches the game as it starts. Systems that only matter later are taught through New entries on the Needs attention list and the Almanac, not through tutorial chapters for later eras.
- **Our own words.** No tutorial text, character name or screen name comes from other strategy games.

If you skip the tutorial and the guided turns, you still get the Needs attention list, tooltips, "Why?" and the Almanac.

### The Year of Varna

The tutorial is played as the Ottomans, from 11 November 1444 to 1447, in ten chapters. Like every campaign it starts the day after Varna: the twelve-year-old Mehmed II is on the throne, his father Murad II has retired to Manisa, and the crusade against the Ottomans is still at war. It keeps history's own dates and never moves an event to suit the lesson plan. Varna itself is never fought again: the tutorial teaches what came after it, with the crusade broken, Murad coming back, the Janissaries wanting their pay and the Danube frontier to hold. The Ottomans are in the free tier, so the whole tutorial works without a purchase on every store and in the Steam demo. Each chapter starts from its own fixed chapter save, so you can play or replay any chapter on its own.

| # | Chapter | Date | Turns | What it teaches | Done when |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | The map | November 1444 | 0 | Pan and zoom through world, region and local view; select a province; open its card; switch lenses (Food, then Faith); find the capital, the Straits and Varna | You have opened Edirne's province card and seen the Food and Faith lenses |
| 1 | A month at a time | November 1444 to January 1445 | 3 | Country card; gold and food at a glance; End turn; the turn report; the first event (Murad goes back to Manisa: let him go, or ask him to stay this winter) | The Manisa event is answered and the turn report read |
| 2 | A boy on the throne | February to April 1445 | 3 | Ruler and heirs; why a child ruler and a new reign make lords waver; the lords Evrenos, Mihal and Turahan; what a lord wants; royal provinces and lords' lands; hiring an official; tax rates | An official is placed, and one wavering lord has been given his want, a gift, or left alone (each is fine; the turn report shows the result) |
| 3 | Bread and castles | May to September 1445 | 1 plus Advance | Food lives in provinces; castles collect and spread it; reach rings; the Food policy screen and presets; the September harvest; ends when news comes that the crusaders' fleet is on the Danube | A preset is chosen, the harvest turn played, and one hungry province's "Why?" opened |
| 4 | Men under arms | September 1445 | 3 | The crusade, its league and its Defend the faith aim; the Faith policy choice for the Orthodox; ferrying troops over the Bosporus; the levy in Thrace; levies against trained regiments; the Janissaries and how they were raised; calling the lords' riders; making an official Marshal; standing orders | An army of levies, lords' riders and Janissaries stands with a commander |

| 6 | Schools and inventions | early 1446 | 2 plus Advance | Madrasas, students and scholars; schools have a faith; a served, protected community on a Faith line; choosing what to study; every student is a recruit the army cannot raise | An invention is chosen and you have seen the recruit pool shrink |

| 8 | The wall at Corinth | November to December 1446 | 4 to 6 | Declaring a war yourself; marching into the Morea; besieging the Hexamilion month by month; storming with guns; terms | The wall falls or terms are agreed |

In all, about 30 to 40 turns and 45 to 50 minutes, in sessions of about 5 minutes. A turn is one week, so each chapter uses Advance to… to skip the quiet weeks between its lessons.

**History and your choices.** When your choices differ from history, the chapter carries on from what actually happened, as a real campaign would. When the next chapter starts, its chapter save puts the world back on its historical course, and its opening card says so in one line. Nothing you did off script carries into a later chapter. Every scripted event keeps the event tree's history block with its sources. The recruiting, battle and siege lessons use their own Ottoman set of worked numbers (for now: 1,000 Janissaries, 4,000 levies, 2,000 lords' riders, a crusader army of 16,000, and a Hexamilion that holds 2 months).

### Pacing tools

- **Advance to…** runs several weekly turns in a row to a named date and shows one combined turn report.
- **Input focus.** During a "do" step only the controls on the step's allowed list respond; everything else is dimmed, not hidden. **Free look**, a small button, lifts the focus for that step.
- **Quiet world.** While the tutorial runs, every computer war against the Ottomans except the scripted ones is held back, and so is every computer war in the Balkans and western Anatolia (Karaman included).
- **Battle at watch speed.** The chapter 5 border clash at Giurgiu always opens the battle screen, slowed to one bell every 3 seconds.

### The tutorial scenario

The tutorial is one scenario file holding its start state, world rules, scripted events, chapter saves and steps. It starts from the normal 11 November 1444 setup, with its own court (Mehmed II on the throne, Murad II at Manisa), and overrides only where the tutorial needs them: the crusade war left open, with the league holding the Defend the faith aim from the start; Çandarlı Halil as grand vizier; the Evrenos, Mihal and Turahan families as lords, one of them wavering; one royal province in Anatolia with no official; a fixed seed; the Christian sipahis' lord card (Wavering); and the Orthodox row of the Faith policy set to Restricted. The 1444 map already gives the Ottomans Edirne and Varna and Byzantium the Morea.

The computer is steered: Hungary crosses the Danube at Giurgiu in chapter 5 with Hunyadi commanding, and the Morea accepts terms after the wall falls. The scripted events are Murad going back to Manisa (November 1444), the crusaders' fleet on the Danube (summer 1445), and the Janissary pay rising with Murad's return (1446). Each is marked tutorial only and never fires in a real campaign.

Chapter saves are rebuilt by a tool whenever rules change, by playing the previous chapter's recommended path in the engine; they are never edited by hand. The same chapter save and the same orders always give the same result. A CI test plays each chapter's recommended path from its save and checks it reaches "Done when".

### Going off script

| If you… | Then… |
|---|---|
| Asked Murad to stay in chapter 1 | He stays at Edirne as a commander until chapter 1 ends; chapter 2 starts from its own save with him back at Manisa |
| Lose the border clash | The step changes to "Your army broke. Fall back behind the walls of Edirne and call the levy." Hungary offers terms after failing to take one castle in two months, so the chapter still ends in peace |
| Ignored the wavering lord | His loyalty drops one step, and the turn report shows it; later chapters start from their own saves |
| Sit idle on a "do" step | After 8 seconds the pointer pulses on the target again; after 20 the note adds "Stuck? Skip this step." |
| Are short of gold at a chapter's start | A Genoese or Venetian banking house fills the gap with a scripted loan, shown as a real debt line |
| Fail the storm in chapter 8 | The siege carries on month by month until the wall is breached or the Morea offers terms |

### The crusade after Varna

The crusade is built on the real [Religion](Religion), [Diplomacy](Diplomacy), [Warfare](Warfare) and [Troop movement](Troop-Movement) rules.

- **Chapter 0.** After the Food lens you switch to the Faith lens: Rumelia shows as Orthodox under a Sunni realm, hatched where a community is 30 % or more, with the line "Most of your people in Rumelia are Orthodox Christians. You rule them as a Sunni sultan."
- **The league.** The crusading league against the Ottomans is at war from the start, regardless of the usual threshold: Hungary, Wallachia and the Papal States as members, with Venice and Burgundy sending fleets. On chapter 3's last turn the news comes that its fleet is on the Danube, and the country card shows "League against the Ottomans: 3 members, 24,000 soldiers". The league holds the Defend the faith aim from the start (a Restricted policy alone would not qualify), and the war screen shows the aim, its cause and one linked line on why this aim and not Take land. Tapping a league member opens its reasons (Same faith +10 between Catholic members; "Our ally's war aim is our concern"), the same screen used for your own alliances.
- **The faith choice (chapter 4).** When the league calls on the sultan's Christian subjects to rise, the vizier opens the Faith policy screen at the Orthodox row, and you choose:

| Choice | What follows |
|---|---|
| Keep it Restricted | The faith tax keeps paying for the war, and the Christian sipahis stay Wavering, so they do not bring their riders when called |
| Tolerate it | The Christian sipahis steady to Loyal and their riders come to the Danube; Pious Sunni lords waver once (the Orthodox are more than 10 % of the realm); the faith tax ends |

  Persecute is shown dimmed, with a note that it would give every Orthodox neighbour the Defend the faith aim and grow the crusade.
- **The Straits.** While the crusaders' fleet holds the Dardanelles, the crossing shows as closed with the fleet's banner on it, so you send Murad's Anatolian troops over the Bosporus ferry: 2 days, plus a day per 10,000 men.
- **After the border clash**, the turn report shows the battle's result together with the faith choice: how many Christian sipahis rode at Giurgiu, or why they stayed home. Peace with Hungary ends the league's war, and the league with it.
- **Chapter 6** opens a Rumelian province's Faith line, showing the Orthodox community as "served, Restricted, protected: no grievance", with "Why?" linking to the religion page. The madrasa in Edirne is Sunni.

The tutorial calls the crusade what it was, frames neither side's holy war as the right one, rewards no harm, and counts the dead of the border clash on both sides the same way.

### The Janissaries

In chapter 4, after the Janissaries join the army, a step opens their regiment card and the "Why?" on its men. The recruits line reads "Boys taken from Christian families in Rumelia (the devshirme), and war captives." Tapping it moves the camera to the Rumelian provinces the corps drew from. Then one illustrated vignette card, in the style of an event and marked narrative only, shows three panels: officials arriving in a village, the boy's years of training, and the soldier he becomes, remembered in his village. It has only Continue and Read more; no option gives you anything. The step ends on a Rumelian province card whose People line shows the boys taken and whose grievances show *Sons taken*, with its Orthodox lords one step lower in loyalty, and the vizier's line "The corps is strong. This is what it costs the villages that fill it." The Almanac's entry is sourced from the catalogue's Ottoman record and standard histories, with no invented numbers.

The tutorial also proposes (not a rule yet) how the devshirme could work in a real campaign for a realm with the *Kapıkulu household* birthright: a collection every 5 years, a *Sons taken* grievance for 10 years, lords wavering, families leaving as refugees, and one lever, ending it, after which the corps refills from paid volunteers at a higher cost.

### Steps and how they look

Each step has an id, a kind, its text (a string key), an anchor, an allowed list, a done condition, whether the guide's face shows, and a length limit of 140 characters.

| Kind | Ends when |
|---|---|
| look | You confirm, or tap anywhere; never auto-advanced |
| do | Its condition is met; there is no Next button |
| choose | You make any choice; the next step reacts to which |
| watch | What it plays (a turn, a battle) ends; it can be paused |

Text tokens: `{btn:…}` becomes the prompt for your current input; `{ui:…}` the screen's name in bold with its icon; `{stat:…}` the stat's icon and name linked to the Almanac; `{place:…}` and `{lord:…}` a linked name that moves the camera there.

The step note is a parchment strip at the bottom edge, never over the target, moving to the top when the target is low, and at most a third of the screen tall. A spotlight with a gold edge sits on the anchor, and the rest of the screen dims to 50 % (not black). The guide's face is the grand vizier Çandarlı Halil, labelled "The Grand Vizier", and he appears only in the tutorial. A new step plays a soft hint sound and a finished step a confirm sound; neither lowers the music. A counter shows the steps of the chapter ("2 / 6"). In the tutorial, the pause menu gains Skip this chapter, Chapter list and Leave the tutorial.

### Guided first turns

If you skipped the tutorial, or when you start your first real campaign after finishing it, a goals list sits under the top bar for the first in-game year:

| Goal | Opens | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| Look over your realm | Country card | Opened |
| Choose how your castles share food | Food policy | A preset or setting chosen |
| Check your lords | Lords list | Opened |
| Put an official in every royal province | Officials | No royal province without one, or dismissed |
| Choose what your scholars study | Research | An invention chosen |
| Know your neighbours | The realms around you, from the country card | Opened |
| End your first turn | End turn | Pressed |

The list folds into a single icon with a count and never covers the map. Each goal's Show me runs the matching tutorial steps in your current campaign, without the tutorial's scenario overrides. Goals adapt to the realm: a realm with no lords skips "Check your lords", and a pastoral realm gets the same food goal as everyone else. Closing the list for good from its own menu hides it for good, and turning off "Tutorial hints" in Settings hides it too.

### First-campaign hints

In your first campaign a small parchment note along the bottom of the screen says what to tap next, and a brass ring pulses around the button or card it means. There are six, one at a time: choose a realm, move an army (Commanders, a commander, then the land to march to), end the turn, recruit (a province's Garrison tab, then Recruit), build (the Build tab, then Build) and send an envoy (a neighbour's Diplomacy tab, then a mission). Doing the thing ticks its hint off, in any order, and the next one appears; an order the game refuses ticks nothing. When what to tap is not on screen, Show me opens the card that holds it. The × dismisses one hint, and Stop hints turns them all off.

The note sits between the overview button and End turn and moves under the top bar when what it points at is low, so it never covers it; the ring takes no taps, so the button under it works as ever. The counter ("3 / 6") shows how far you are. Hints wait while an event card is open or a week plays. Which hints are done stays with you, not with the save: a second campaign picks up where the first left off, and once all six are done they never come back. In Settings (the cog in the side tools), Gameplay holds the Tutorial hints switch with "n of 6 done" and "Reset tutorial and hints", which brings all six back.

### Needs attention

There is no advisor in a real campaign; the vizier belongs to the tutorial. Instead, the Needs attention screen holds one-line entries, each with a Show button that moves the camera or opens the screen. It has two groups: **Warnings** first, then **Waiting for you**, which holds opportunities and then New entries. Opportunity lines state a fact; they never tell you what to do.

| Kind | Check | Example line |
|---|---|---|
| Warning | A province's store will reach zero before the next harvest | "Sofia will run out of bread by March." |
| Warning | An army's food reserve is under 15 days | "Murad's army has twelve days of food left." |
| Warning | A lord turns Wavering or Disloyal | "Turahan Bey grows cold toward you. He wants a war on the Morea." |
| Warning | Pay owed to soldiers | "Your soldiers have not been paid for two months." |
| Warning | Gold runs out in under three months at this rate | "At this pace the treasury is empty by June." |
| Warning | A harvest is near and many farmers are levied | "Five thousand farmers are under arms with the harvest three weeks away." |
| Warning | Grievances pass the rising threshold | "Talk of rising in Edirne." |
| Warning | A royal province has no official | "Bursa pays half its taxes without an official." |
| Opportunity | A neighbour's disloyal lord borders you | "A lord across the border may be won over." |
| Opportunity | A castle has more grain than it can hold | "Edirne has more than it can hold." |
| New | The first war, siege, battle, rising, famine or plague | One line, linked to the Almanac |
| New | The first invention of a new era; a new age begins | One line on what changes |

All checks live in one config file and are read each turn after that turn's simulation; in the tutorial the same checks drive the vizier's notes. An entry leaves when its cause ends or you dismiss it, and a dismissed entry does not return before its check's cooldown ends (a province's famine warning at most once a year). Long-press, right-click or the info button on an entry offers "Mute this" (just that line) and "Don't list this kind again"; you can undo any mute in Settings. A new warning plays the alert sound, without lowering the music. The End turn button shows a small count of warnings, and the turn report shows one line, "Needs attention (3)", that opens the list rather than repeating it. Each later system adds its own warnings and first-time moments as its design lists them.

### Tooltips, "Why?" and linked words

- **Tooltip:** the stat's name and one line on what it is. Turn on "Detailed tooltips" in Settings and the tooltip also holds the full "Why?" list. Long-press on touch, hover after the settings delay with a mouse, or focus then the info button on a controller.
- **"Why?":** the full gain and lose list with this month's numbers and an Almanac link. Tap or click the number, or focus then confirm. Every number on every card has a "Why?"; a lint test checks that every stat key has a tooltip line and an Almanac entry.
- **Linked words:** the first time a game term appears in a text (event options, tooltips, the Needs attention list, the turn report), it is linked to its Almanac entry with a dotted underline. A glossary file maps each term and its plurals per language.

### The Almanac

The in-game encyclopedia, fully offline, opened from the pause menu, any link or any "Why?". It uses the same parchment, ribbons and pixel icons as the cards, takes its numbers from config (so a balance patch updates it), and is searchable with a text box, or a letter wheel on a controller.

| Section | Contents | How it is written |
|---|---|---|
| How to play | One page per system, matching the chapters, each with Practice this (opens that chapter) | By hand, short |
| Stats | What each stat is, its gain and lose list, which screens show it | Generated from config, with a hand-written opening line |
| Inventions | Each invention, what it needs, what it unlocks | Generated from research data |
| Troops and commanders | Arms, kinds, commander styles, by era | Generated from warfare data |
| Ground | The 22 tile types and their effects on troops, buildings and people | Generated from terrain data |
| Realms | Every country met so far: short history, strengths | Generated from the catalogue; hidden until met |
| Events seen | Events fired in this campaign, with their history block | Generated from the event tree |

Loading-screen tips come from a `tips` list that links each tip to its Almanac entry, chosen from what you have not opened yet.

### Inputs

The game tracks the last input you touched and swaps every prompt live, even mid-step. Controller glyphs come by family (Xbox, PlayStation, Steam Controller and Steam Deck, a generic set), and from Steam Input on Steam. With a controller, every panel is navigable with the d-pad, focus starts on the button the step wants, and the spotlight follows focus. The tutorial needs no text entry. Chapter 0 has a different script per input, asking you to try each camera action once; later chapters use one script with tokens.

| Action | Touch | Mouse and keyboard | Controller |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pan the map | Drag | Drag, or WASD and arrows | Left stick moves the cursor; the map follows at the edge |
| Zoom | Pinch; double-tap to zoom in | Wheel; + and − | Right stick up and down |
| Select | Tap | Click | A / Cross |
| Back, close | Close button | Esc, right-click on a panel | B / Circle |
| Info | Long-press | Hover; I | Y / Triangle |
| Action menu | Action menu button on the selection | Right-click; Space | X / Square |
| Move an army | Tap army, tap Move, tap target | Select, right-click target | Select, X for Move, cursor to target, A |
| Cycle my armies | Tap the army count in the top bar | Tab | LB and RB |
| Cycle lenses | Lens button, then pick | L | LT and RT |
| Country card | The realm's pennant in the top bar | C | View / Share |
| End turn | Hourglass, lower right | Enter | Hold Menu / Options for half a second |
| Pause menu | Menu button | Esc with nothing open | Menu / Options, short press |
| Skip a tutorial step | Skip step on the note | Skip step on the note | Hold B / Circle for one second |

### Starting, skipping and returning

The first time you tap to start, the game asks "New to Pocket Hegemony?" with Play the tutorial (recommended), Guided campaign and I know the way. The main menu has a chapter list with a tick on each chapter done, and any chapter can be played at any time. Tutorial progress (chapters done, the current step, mutes, first-time entries seen) is kept in the save. The chapter saves that ship with the game are read-only.

At the end of chapter 9, "Keep ruling the Ottomans" turns the tutorial save into a normal campaign: the quiet world lifts, scripted events stop, the vizier's notes give way to the Needs attention list, and the save is marked "began as the tutorial", so it is not ironman. The tutorial earns no achievements.

### Accessibility and language

The note's text follows the UI scale and may take up to two lines more at the largest size. The chapter 0 lens lesson uses the Food lens, whose hungry provinces also carry the hunger icon, so it works without telling colours apart. With reduced motion on, the hand does not bob and the spotlight does not pulse, and the border clash can be skipped to its result like any battle. All tutorial, Needs attention and Almanac text lives in string tables with keys, tokens stay inside the strings, and the step length check runs per language.

### Measuring

Only with your consent, the game sends one event per tutorial step reached, skipped or finished, and per Needs attention entry followed or muted, and nothing else. Offline, these are queued and dropped after 30 days.
