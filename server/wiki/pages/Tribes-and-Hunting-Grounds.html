Tribes and hunting grounds

The peoples of North America cannot colonise, so a tribe starts with two or three provinces and empty land all round it. Hunting grounds give it room to grow: a tribe walks a few unowned provinces next to its land, lives off them, and after years makes them its own. Every number here is provisional and lives in tribes.json.

#Which realms are tribes

A realm is a tribal realm while its form is a clan council, a chiefdom or a confederation and its capital lies in North America. At the 1378 start that includes the Iroquois and the Huron; the Cherokee and the Creek are not born yet and form later (see The 1378 start). The Pueblo are a city commune: their towns grew by farming, so they walk no grounds unless a reform makes them a tribal form. A realm that reforms out of a tribal form loses its grounds at once.

At the start each tribe fills its range with the unowned land nearest its own, the most peopled first, walking out one province at a time; the tribes take their turn in map order, so a province the Cherokee took first is not the Creek's.

#Claiming and leaving

Open the province card of an unowned land province touching your land or one of your grounds and tap Claim as hunting ground · 3 months. After three months it is yours to walk. One claim runs at a time, and only while your range has a free place. When the button is greyed, the reason is written under it: no free place, a claim is under way, it is another tribe's ground, or a colony is being founded there.

Leave this ground (it asks first) frees the place; you may not claim that province again for five years. A ground is lost when it gets an owner (a colony, a treaty, a war) or, at the next month, when it no longer touches your land or another of your grounds.

#What a ground gives

  • Furs and hides: 0.5 gold a month for each ground, on its own line of the Treasury ledger.
  • Food: your armies and your allies' refill their wagons there as in your own land; an enemy's army strips it as enemy land.
  • Neighbours: two tribes whose grounds touch are neighbours, which counts for the confederation reform's "allied with 2 neighbours of the same people".

A ground stays unowned in every other way: it pays no tax, needs no official, raises no levy and does not count toward world domination.

#Taking them in

A ground you have held for ten years can be taken in while you are at peace and no colony is being founded there: it becomes your province, with its people, their tongue and faith, and its place in the range is free again. If its people speak your tongue, a clan council or chiefdom needs no official there; otherwise it needs one, counted against your officials cap.

#Settlers on our land

A colony founded on a tribe's ground meets fiercer locals (one step: Peaceful becomes Wary, Wary Hostile, Hostile Fierce), and the tribe holds the wrong Settlers on our land (−30, fading over 20 years) and the war aim Punish against the founder for 10 years. A tribal player gets an alert over the map with Go there.

A colonial power can instead ask for the land: from the province card, the tribe's Diplomacy tab or the fleet card's Colony section, offering 10 gold or more. The tribe answers a month later by its reasons count:

#The Hunting grounds tab

A tribal realm's card has a Hunting grounds tab in the Lands group, beside Colonies: settlers on your grounds at the top in red with Go there, asks for your land, "Hunting grounds n of m" with a bar, the claim under way and its end date, then one row per ground with its people, years held and furs, a ground ready to take in first. When settlers or asks wait, the Lands tile on your Realm book's Overview turns red and opens it.

On the map, grounds are a light diagonal hatch in the tribe's colour at every zoom, in the political and Charts lenses. In the Charts lens an empty coast that is someone's hunting ground is amber, not green, so you see before sailing that settling there angers a tribe.

#The computer's tribes

Each month a tribe of the computer with a free place claims the most peopled province it may; each January it takes in every ground it may, oldest first, while within its officials cap. A colonial power of the computer that holds twice the price asks for the land before settling on a ground, and founds there only after the answer.
