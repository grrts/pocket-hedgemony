The 1378 Start

Every campaign begins on 20 September 1378, the day the Western Schism begins. The world is built from research: every realm that existed that autumn, from great kingdoms to stateless societies, put onto the map with its ruler, its government, its strengths and weaknesses, its friends and enemies, and the goals it was really pursuing. Realms that were not yet born wait asleep and can form later. This page explains what the world looks like on the first day, which realms come later, how history guides the computer realms without forcing them, how a country's starting gold, soldiers and officials are worked out, how each province gets its starting people, wealth and castles, which realms you can play, and which campaign rules you pick before you begin. Once the first turn starts, other systems take over.

#How it works

#The start day: 20 September 1378

On 20 September 1378 the cardinals meeting at Fondi elect Clement VII against Urban VI, the Pope they had chosen in Rome that spring. From that day Latin Christendom has two Popes, and every Catholic realm must decide which one it obeys. That is why the campaign starts here: the first years hand almost every realm a choice.

Some things have already happened by the start day:

  • On the Vozha (11 August 1378) Moscow has beaten Mamai's Horde for the first time.
  • The Ciompi have risen in Florence, and a regime of the minor guilds rules the city.
  • Tokhtamysh has taken the White Horde with Timur's help.
  • Ayutthaya has made Sukhothai its vassal.

What history did next, and what your rivals will be reaching for:

None of this is forced. Each of these is something a realm wants, and whether it happens depends on what you and the other realms do (see History is a reference).

The campaign runs from 20 September 1378 to 31 August 1939, one week per turn: about 29,250 turns. The tutorial, Murad's Frontier, starts on the same day, playing the Ottomans under Murad I (see Tutorial and Help).

#The world on 20 September 1378

About 315 realms hold land on the map at the start, many more than in 1444, because the world of 1378 is more divided. These are the big stories you start in the middle of.

The Church: two Popes. Urban VI sits in Rome and Clement VII goes to Avignon. Each Catholic realm holds an obedience to one of them, and its Pope is the one whose censure, patronage and calls count for it. On the start day 150 realms and lords follow Rome: the Empire, England, Hungary and Poland, Scandinavia and most of Italy. 30 follow Avignon: France and its great fiefs, Scotland, Naples, Savoy, Provence, Lorraine, Cyprus and the Knights of Rhodes. 22 have not chosen yet, among them Castile, Aragon, Portugal and Navarre. In history Castile went to Avignon in 1381 and Aragon in 1387. The Council of Pisa (1409) can add a third Pope, and the Council of Constance (1414 to 1418) can end the Schism. There are no Hussites yet: Hus is not burned until 1415.

The Empire. Charles IV of the house of Luxembourg is Emperor and King of Bohemia, aged 62. His son Wenceslaus, aged 17, is already King of the Romans and follows him without an election. The seven electors are Mainz, Trier, Cologne, Bohemia, the Palatinate, Saxe-Wittenberg and Brandenburg, which the Luxembourgs also hold, so the house has two votes. Meissen and Thuringia belong to the Wettins, who are not electors yet, and Nuremberg is still only a burgraviate of the Hohenzollerns. Austria is shared by the brothers Albert III and Leopold III. See Institutions.

The Hundred Years' War, its Caroline phase. Charles V of France and his constable Bertrand du Guesclin are winning back the land England took. England's king, Richard II, is 11. England and France are at war; Castile fights beside France at sea and against Navarre, whose Norman lands Charles V has just seized. England keeps Calais and Gascony, Scotland keeps an uneasy truce with England, and Brittany under John IV is France's most restless vassal.

Italy. Venice and Genoa are at war over Tenedos and the straits: the War of Chioggia. Padua, Hungary and the Patriarch of Aquileia join Genoa, while Bernabò Visconti of Milan backs Venice. The Visconti lands are split between Bernabò at Milan and his nephew Gian Galeazzo at Pavia. Joanna I rules Naples and backs Avignon.

Hungary and Poland under Louis. Louis the Great is king of both, aged 52, with no son. His daughter Mary, aged 7, is heir. Poland is governed for him by his mother Elizabeth, and its lords want a king of their own. Hungary holds Wallachia, Bosnia, Vidin and Ragusa as subjects and is at war with Venice.

The north and east. Lithuania is still pagan, ruled by Jogaila beside his uncle Kęstutis, and at war with the Teutonic and Livonian Orders, whose crusades raid it every year. Moscow under Dmitry Ivanovich, aged 27, has stopped paying tribute to Mamai and is at war with him. Tver, Ryazan and Nizhny Novgorod still pay the Horde. Albert of Mecklenburg is King of Sweden, and a child, Olaf II, is King of Denmark.

The Hordes, Tokhtamysh and Timur. The Ulus of Jochi is broken in three. Mamai, a beylerbey who is not of Genghis Khan's line, holds the west through puppet khans and wants to punish Moscow. Arab Shah holds part of the Volga. Tokhtamysh, khan of the White Horde since this year, wants Sarai and the whole Ulus. Behind him stands Timur, Amir of the Chagatai ulus since 1370, aged 42, who shelters and arms him. Iran is split among five successor states (the Jalayirids at Tabriz and Baghdad, the Muzaffarids, the Kartids of Herat, the Sarbadars and the Sufids of Khwarazm) and Timur means to take them.

Murad's Ottomans and the beyliks. The Ottoman Beylik of Murad I, aged 52, is a rising frontier state on both sides of the straits. Byzantium is its tributary, and Ivan Shishman of Tarnovo, Marko of Prilep and Constantine Dragaš are its vassals. In Anatolia the beyliks still stand: Karaman (the Ottomans' great rival), Germiyan, Aydın, Menteşe, Saruhan, Hamid, Teke, Candar and the Eretnids. In the Balkans Lazar's Serbia, the Lands of Vuk Branković, the Balšić Zeta, two Bulgarian tsardoms (Tarnovo and Vidin) and Dobruja stand between the Ottomans and Hungary. Kosovo is still ahead.

The Byzantine civil war. Andronikos IV holds Constantinople and his father John V and brother Manuel are his prisoners. He owes his throne to Murad. In history John V retakes the city in July 1379, again with Ottoman help. The Despotate of the Morea holds the south of Greece, beside Latin Achaea and Catalan Athens.

The Mamluks rule Egypt and Syria under a boy sultan, al-Mansur Ali II, hold Jerusalem, and protect the holy cities of the Hejaz.

South Asia. Firuz Shah Tughlaq, aged 69, rules a Delhi sultanate far larger than the one of 1444: it still holds Gujarat, Malwa and Multan, and Sindh pays it tribute. Bengal is free and at peace with Delhi. In the Deccan the Bahmanis and Vijayanagar under Harihara II are rivals.

East Asia. The Ming under the Hongwu Emperor, aged 50, are ten years old. They are still at war with the Northern Yuan on the steppe and with Naghachu in Manchuria, and a Yuan prince still holds Yunnan. Goryeo, not yet Joseon, rules Korea under King U, aged 13. Japan has two courts at war: the Ashikaga shogun Yoshimitsu, aged 20, and the weakening Southern Court.

Southeast Asia. Champa under Chế Bồng Nga has sacked Thăng Long and is at war with Dai Viet. Majapahit under Hayam Wuruk holds tribute from much of the archipelago and the Malay peninsula, Singapura among them. Ayutthaya has just made Sukhothai its vassal.

Africa. Mali under Mansa Musa II still rules the western Sahel. Ethiopia under Newaya Maryam is at war with Ifat. The Bulala are driving the Sayfawa out of Kanem.

The Americas. In the valley of Mexico the Tepanecs of Azcapotzalco under Tezozómoc are the great power, and the Mexica of Tenochtitlan, under their first tlatoani Acamapichtli, are their tributaries. In the Andes Cusco is one small lordship among its rivals, and Chimor at Chan Chan is the strong power of the coast. Mayapan still rules in Yucatán.

#Realms that come later

A realm that was not yet born in 1378 is a sleeping realm. It is listed from the start but hidden and landless until it wakes. 67 realms sleep on the start day, among them:

  • Europe: Spain, the Dutch Republic, Wales, Transylvania, Poland-Lithuania and Russia, and Albanian lordships such as the Kastrioti and the League of Lezhë.
  • The steppe: Kazan, the Crimea, the Nogais, the Uzbek Khanate and the Oirats.
  • South Asia: Jaunpur, Gujarat, Malwa, Multan, Kabul, the Mughal Empire and the Sikh Empire.
  • Southeast and East Asia: Malacca, and Japan's great warrior houses (the Hosokawa, Ōuchi, Takeda, Date, Shimazu and others).
  • Africa: Kongo, Mutapa, Adal, Ajuran, Kaffa, Buganda, Rwanda and the Aïr Sultanate.
  • North America: the Cherokee, the Creek and Coosa.

A sleeping realm wakes when its conditions hold: by a decision, which you or a computer realm can take once it holds the land and shares the faith and tongue, or by an event. A computer realm usually forms one when its goals lead there, near the historical date, but it stays formable for the whole campaign. Its record from the 1444 catalogue is its blueprint: its name, colour, capital and home land. If you never take the steps, Spain may never be.

#History is a reference

History in Pocket Hegemony is a reference, not a script. No event is forced by its date. History still tends to run its normal course, because every realm pursues the goals it really had, for the reasons it had them.

Each goal names what the realm wants, why, when it is likeliest and when the goal ends. Some goals from the start data:

  • A goal ends when its reason does. If Tokhtamysh is dead, Timur stops chasing him. If the Ottomans already hold Niš, that goal is done.
  • The computer weighs goals like any other wish. A goal makes a war aim, an alliance, a marriage or a decision more attractive while its reason holds and its time is near. It never makes a realm do what it can't afford: a weak Timur doesn't march on Anatolia.
  • Realms sometimes go their own way. At the start of each campaign the seed shakes every realm's goals. A goal can be eager in one campaign and half-hearted in the next; some goals are dropped or swapped for a plausible alternative (the Ottomans may turn on Anatolia first, Venice may look to the mainland); a new ruler may bring a goal of his own. The big picture usually holds, but no two campaigns run the same.
  • The events choice sets how closely history is followed. Historical leans harder on history, Plausible (the default) is in between, and Free loosens it.
  • You are never steered. Your own realm has no goals forced on it. Other realms' goals show on their realm card, so you can see what Florence or Timur is after.

#Research and the eras

The research tree keeps its eras, and Era I is stretched back to the start: it runs from 1378 to 1500. The Medieval era ends in 1377, and inventions from before then stay "known before the start". Era I is now 122 years long, so its inventions cost more and a typical realm still reaches Era II around 1500.

A few start inventions differ from 1444, because they had not spread yet. The Ottomans don't know bombards, gunpowder infantry or tax registers. Bohemia and Hungary have no wagon fort (that comes with the Hussites). Portugal and Castile don't have the carrack and caravel, while Genoa and Venice do. Ming keeps its handgunners but has no tax registers until the Yellow Registers of 1381. Timur's realm has no observatory, while the Jalayirids keep the Maragha tradition. Goryeo knows metal movable type. See Research.

#The 1444 checkpoint

The world of 11 November 1444, the day after the battle of Varna, is still in the game as the main history checkpoint. Its map and catalogue are the reference that a test run from 1378 is compared with: who holds the key places, which realms exist and who the great powers are. Eight other dates are checked beside it, from Timur's death in 1405 to Sarajevo in 1914. The check runs only when the designer asks; the game itself never corrects a realm that wanders off history.

A tester can still open the 1444 world by adding ?start=1444 to the address. A campaign you began in 1444 keeps playing in its own world: opening its save reopens the game in 1444 and loads it, and going back to the start screen returns to 1378 for new campaigns.

#The country catalogue

The starting world comes from the country catalogue: 963 researched records of the political entities of the 1440s, and a 1378 pass over them region by region. The 1378 pass dates each record, makes the realms born later sleeping realms, and adds records for the realms of 1378 that were gone by 1444, such as the Hordes, the Iranian successor states, the Anatolian beyliks, the Bulgarian tsardoms, Padua, Verona, Goryeo, the Tepanecs and Mayapan. The catalogue is history, not game balance: it holds no numeric game statistics at all. Population appears only as a published range with a source, and army sizes or budgets only when they are directly sourced.

Who gets in. The catalogue follows a few inclusion rules:

  • Date test: an entity is included only if it existed on the start day with the identity recorded. Entities founded later become sleeping realms or wait for events.
  • Agency test: subordinate entities are included when their internal politics mattered. Pure administrative subdivisions are excluded_not_distinct or merged_into_other.
  • Groups: where hundreds of micro-polities existed, the significant ones get their own record and the rest go into a named group record (such as silesia or tusi_group), which never implies a single state.
  • No forced states: societies without a state are recorded as such (stateless, segmentary lineage and so on), as a decentralised polity or a non-state actor, never forced into a nation-state.
  • Disputes are kept: where existence or classification is disputed, the record is kept as included_disputed with the dispute described, never resolved silently.

Ids. Every record has a stable snake_case id that is never reused (for example ottoman, mamluk, chimor). A renamed id goes into an alias list. Relationship edges live in separate relationship files, never inside records: a hierarchical edge is stored with the subordinate party and a symmetric one with party a.

Nothing from commercial games. The catalogue carries no tags, statistics, values, mechanic names or text from commercial games.

Every record carries every section, whatever its importance: region, names, classification, existence, identity, leadership, government, geography with capital, society, economy, military, diplomacy, gameplay data, event links, sources, confidence and open questions. Each record belongs to exactly one of 29 regions, by its core or seat.

Detail tiers set only how much prose a record has: A for major powers, B for significant secondary states, C for minor ones.

Instead of numbers, each record has a qualitative assessment on a set of dimensions, each one very low, low, moderate, high, very high or unknown, with a justification and a confidence. There is deliberately no overall power score.

These are tendencies the AI reads, not scripts. The realm's goals (see History is a reference) grow from them.

#Start forms of government

Every country starts with its historical form of government, assigned by rules tried in order:

  1. Hand-set overrides (75 of them, each with a reason), which win over everything else. Examples: the Ottomans as a household realm, the Mamluks as soldier emirs, Venice as a merchant republic, England as a diet monarchy, Hungary as an elective monarchy.
  2. Text rules (12), tried in order on the lower-cased government text in the catalogue, honouring each rule's allowed and skipped entity types.
  3. Entity type (52 entries). A record of type kingdom takes its form from its region:
RegionKingdom starts as

No country ever starts as an absolute monarchy, constitutional monarchy, constitutional republic, military junta, party state or company rule. Those forms are reached later by reform, revolution or events (see Government). The summary of start forms is generated by a tool, never edited by hand.

Other starting facts:

  • Rulers: every realm starts with its real ruler of 20 September 1378, with his age, his years on the throne and his heir: Murad I, Charles V, the boy Richard II, Louis the Great, Timur, Hongwu. See Your Realm.
  • Relations: allies, rivals, subjects and enemies come straight from the 1378 relations data: 503 relations and 128 facts on 20 September 1378, with each Catholic realm's obedience. Wars under way include England against France, Venice against Genoa and its allies, Mamai against Moscow, Lithuania against the Orders, Champa against Dai Viet, Ming against the Northern Yuan, and Japan's two courts. See Diplomacy and Subjects.
  • Lords: for major countries, lords come from the great houses the catalogue's history names. A country with only one province has no lords. See Your Realm.
  • Traders: starting traders follow trade access (very high 6, high 4, moderate 2, low 1, very low or unknown 0); trade ships for coastal countries with ports, caravans or barges inland. Starting trade ratios are set by running the starting traders for 24 months before the first turn, so busy roads start busy. Genoa's Black Sea trade through Caffa and Tana and Venice's routes are at their peak. See Trade.
  • Faiths: each record's ruler's faith, its people's majority faith and other communities come from the catalogue. Lithuania and Samogitia keep their own Baltic faith until their baptisms. See Religion.
  • Exploration: no realm can explore at the start. Genoa and Venice know the carrack, but the Age of Exploration has not begun; Portugal and Castile must learn the ocean ships first. See Exploration and Colonies.

#The 1378 map today

The 1378 map is built from five regional parts (western Europe, the east, Asia, Africa and the Americas, and Arabia), joined into one start:

  • About 315 map nations, each with a capital, a colour and commanders with might, cunning and logistics (for example France's Bertrand du Guesclin, England's Sir Robert Knolles).
  • Owner rules hand provinces to nations, checked in order, first match wins, by modern country, region name or a longitude and latitude box. Borders are an approximation of 1378, not an atlas. The borders that differ most from 1444 are in Anatolia (the beyliks), Iran (five successor states), the steppe (the Hordes), North India (Delhi still holds Gujarat, Malwa and Multan), France (English Gascony and Calais, Brittany, the Navarrese lands), the Low Countries, Italy, Mexico (the Tepanecs) and the Andes (a small Cusco and a strong Chimor).
  • A realm added from the catalogue whose capital lies in another realm's province is left as reference data (for example Timbuktu in Mali's land).
  • Each nation's colour is one of 32 palette colours not taken by a neighbouring nation.

Population of a province, in thousands of people:

  • Land area times the density of the first matching settlement region (default 0.8 people per km²),
  • ×0.35 in mountains, desert and tundra; ×1.3 in farmland and marsh; ×2.5 for a capital,
  • spread by 0.7 to 1.3 per province,
  • unowned land capped at twice the default density.

Wealth of a province:

  • The first matching settlement region's wealth (default 1; always 1 for unowned land),
  • +1 if owned and coastal, +2 for a capital,
  • spread by 0.75 to 1.25 and kept between 1 and 10.

Settlement regions range from dense, rich ones (the Low Countries at 30 people per km² and wealth 6) to sparse ones (Africa at 3 and 2).

A realm the catalogue does not assess starts with the engine's 50 gold, every province's levies at their ceiling, and 20 % horse in starting armies (all placeholders). A realm's men under arms all start in armies under a commander, spread over its land: one army per commander, the first in the capital. It gets one commander for every 8,000 men, at least one and at most eight (more only if an army would pass 30,000).

#From catalogue to map

A mapping plan says which catalogue record owns which provinces, with capitals, colours, rulers and commanders. The nations on the map are the realms. Where a map nation's id differs from the catalogue (for example the Mexica are Tenochtitlan and Chimú is Chimor), a rename table links the two. A catalogue record that owns no province on the map is background only, and land nobody owns stays free to settle.

A colony can be founded only in a province no country owns. Land of a tribe that is a country (a chiefdom or clan council in the start list) can only be taken by war or treaty.

#Good realms to start with

  • The Ottomans (free): Murad I's frontier state, with Byzantium as a tributary in civil war, Balkan lords as vassals, the beyliks of Anatolia to win by marriage, purchase or war, and Lazar's Serbia and Kosovo ahead. This is the tutorial's realm.
  • France (free): Charles V and Du Guesclin, winning back the land England took, with Brittany and Navarre to settle and an English boy king across the sea.
  • Muscovy: fresh from the Vozha, with Mamai coming for revenge and Tver and Ryazan to master.
  • Venice or Genoa: the War of Chioggia, decided at sea in the first two years.
  • Hungary: two crowns, a ring of Balkan subjects and no son to inherit them.
  • Lithuania: a pagan realm between the crusading Orders and Moscow, with a crown and a baptism on offer.
  • Timur or the White Horde: Timur's rise to empire, or Tokhtamysh's road to Sarai, and the break between them.
  • Ming: a young dynasty still fighting the Yuan on three frontiers.
  • The Mexica: a small tributary city of the Tepanecs that can become an empire.

#Playable realms and the free tier

  • The free tier offers the Ottomans and France. The tutorial is played as the Ottomans.
  • Without the full game, the realm picker shows other realms with a padlock and a button to the Unlock screen. Coming back from a purchase keeps the realm you chose.
  • Every realm on the map is playable. A sleeping realm can't be picked at the start; you form it in play.

#Campaign rules

At new game you set the campaign rules: difficulty, AI temper, fog, historical, plausible or free events, ironman, and the end date (default 31 August 1939). Presets fill the whole step at once. The rules are saved with the campaign, can't be changed afterwards, and are shown read-only under Pause, Campaign rules.

A new game starts from a seed, your nation and the options, and gets a random campaign id. The same seed and the same rules always give the same starting world, including how each realm's goals are shaken. Each save header records your nation and whether it needs the full game. Loading a save of a locked realm asks you to unlock it; the save is never deleted.

#What you see

  • Realm picker: the "Choose your realm" sheet over the map, titled Autumn 1378. It opens on the suggested realms: the great powers of each part of the world and a start for each part of Africa (Mali, Benin, Tio, Ethiopia and Kilwa; Kongo is not founded yet). Or tap any realm on the map. A realm's card names its ruler on the start day and says the campaign starts on 20 September 1378. Locked realms show a padlock and a button to Unlock.
  • Region: the select at the top of the sheet lists every region that has realms, with how many: Europe, the Middle East, North Africa, Sub-Saharan Africa, South Asia, Central and North Asia, East Asia, Southeast Asia, North America, Mesoamerica and Central America, and South America. Choosing a region frames its realms on the map, and the sheet lists every realm of the region from A to Z, one row each with its pennant, name, size and its standing at the start. A realm of one province is never only a speck to hit: tap its row. Regions follow the event tree's macro-regions; the Sahel, West, Central, East and Southern Africa (Ethiopia and the Somali coast too) are Sub-Saharan Africa, the Maghreb, Egypt and the Sahara are North Africa.
  • The realm's card: tapping a row, or one of a realm's provinces on the map, shows the realm at a glance: its ruler, its form of government, provinces, people and what it is strong in, and one line Ways to grow, for example "Ways to grow: 3 smaller neighbours · 4 free provinces · 1 port". It counts the realms next to it by land that have fewer provinces (tributaries it could make), the free land that borders it, and its trade at the start: gold fields, trade and caravan towns, and ports. Rule starts the game as that realm; ✕ goes back to the list.
  • On a phone held sideways the sheet sits at the left with the map beside it; held upright, or in a narrow window, the sheet sits along the bottom under the map. Every row and button is at least 44 points high and nothing needs hover.
  • Campaign rules step: difficulty, AI temper, fog, events, ironman and end date, with presets.
  • Quick start: skips to Begin with the last options used and the realm last tapped (or the first suggested one).
  • Pause, Campaign rules: the rules of the running campaign, read-only.

The screens themselves are on Screens and Navigation.

#How the start is settled

  • The date. Every campaign, and the tutorial, starts on 20 September 1378, the day the Western Schism begins. Murad I rules the Ottomans, Charles IV is Emperor, and Louis the Great is king of Hungary and Poland (decision X-25).
  • History. History is a reference, not a script: realms pursue their historical goals for their reasons, and nothing is forced by its date.
  • Later realms. Every realm not yet born in 1378 is a sleeping realm, formable by decision or woken by an event, with its 1444 record as the blueprint.
  • Eras. Era I is stretched back to the start, 1378 to 1500, and the tree keeps its eras.
  • The 1444 world. 11 November 1444 stays as the main history checkpoint, and testers can open it with ?start=1444.
  • Provinces. People, wealth and castles come from the map.
  • Strengths and reach. Each realm carries its record's assessments and heritage, shown on its card's Heritage tab (National Strengths), and its administrative capacity as a rank from 1 to 5 (0 without a record), which subjects will read for how far its rule reaches.
  • Treasury, levies, officials and envoys. Each realm's starting gold, debt, gathered levies, officials and envoys at court come from its assessment; the flat 50 gold and full levies apply only to a realm without one.
  • Relations. Alliances, rivalries, wars and obediences at the start come from the relations list.
  • Events. "Historical" events follow history closely, "plausible" ones allow likely changes and "free" ones loosen it further.
  • Commanders. Commander numbers come from the map's nation data, so Du Guesclin has Might 5.
  • Minor countries. Small countries start with no great houses.
  • Warm-up. The trade warm-up before the first turn uses the campaign's seed, changes only trade, and does not move the date.

#Still open

  • Goals for the whole world. Goals are written so far for the Balkans, Anatolia, the Caucasus, the Rus lands, the Baltic, the steppe and Iran: 146 goals for 83 realms. The other regions follow.
  • Arabia is still a stand-in taken from the 1444 data.
  • The assessment factors and the ±20 % range are first values that balancing may change.

  • Features: domain/scenario-setup/features/ (catalogue, start-forms, country-start, province-start, map-setup, catalogue-to-map, new-campaign).
  • Code and data: the start is a scenario folder, packages/rules/data/scenarios/1378/, with its day and eras in scenario.json. Each region's part lives in regions/<key>/; tools/domain-data/build-1378.py joins them into map-nations.json, map-owners.json, rulers.json, culture-start.json, relations.json, subjects.json, goals.json and sleeping-realms.json, and tools/mapgen/mapgen.ts 1378 builds world/ from those. packages/rules/src/start.ts picks the scenario; the 1444 checkpoint is packages/rules/data/scenarios/1444/ (?start=1444, or PH_START=1444 in Node, which the test configs pin). Start values the engine uses today are in packages/rules/data/defines.json; start forms come from government/tools/assign_start_forms.py and government/config/start-forms.yaml in the designs. Rules code for this context goes in packages/engine/src/domain/scenario-setup/.
