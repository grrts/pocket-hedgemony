Recruiting

Recruiting is how your realm turns the people of its provinces into soldiers. You call men up with one slider (the levy), train them into regiments of one troop type at a castle or drill yard, keep the best as standing regiments, and take officials out of their provinces to lead your armies. Every step has a price in gold, food, work at home and goodwill, and this page explains each one.

#How it works

#Who can be called

Only fighting-age men can be called, and they are a quarter of a province's people, in every realm. The levy calls men only. Each class gives at most a share of its fighting-age men, its call share:

ClassCall share
Homeless24 %
Herders16 %
Farmers12 %
Woodsmen12 %
Miners8 %
Seafolk8 %
Craftsmen8 %
Market folk6 %
Clerks4 %
Students4 %
Clergy and scholars0

Add these up over a province's classes and you get its men who can be called, the ceiling. The ceiling is hard: no levy, war or invention ever takes a province past it. In Troyes that works out at about one man in eight of the fighting-age men. Students can be called from the same pool at the clerks' share (1,000 students give 10 men), while clergy and scholars cannot. Students who are called stop making research.

The class a man comes from does not change the soldier he becomes. Every levied man arrives as levy infantry, the weakest troop type, with farm tools. Classes only matter for how many men you can call and what you lose at home when they leave.

With the invention W15 Mass conscription every class's call share rises by half (homeless 36 %, farmers 18 % and so on), and the ceiling stays a share of the fighting-age men.

#The levy

Tap one of your provinces, then its Garrison tab, and press Recruit 1,000 men once for each thousand you want. Under the button you see what that thousand is as a share of the province's men who can be called, and when all the thousands queued there will have gathered; when the button can't be pressed it says why (queue full, too few men, over your troop cap, not enough gold). Up to 10 thousands wait in a province's queue, one row each with the days left, and the × on the last row takes it back with its levy money and men. There is no levy for a whole region or the country: you recruit one province at a time. The costs below are the design's full levy screen; the game shows the levy money on the button.

  • Gold now: levy money of 20 gold per 1,000 men, paid up front for food on the road.
  • Food now: while they gather, levies eat 1 ration a man a month from the province's local store.
  • Pay: free for the first 3 months, then 0.15 gold per man a month.
  • Harvest: how much of the harvest you lose by taking farmers.
  • Pool left: how much of each province's recruit pool remains.
  • Ready in: when each thousand will be ready. The men gather a thousand at a time, 14 days for each thousand (provisional), so 10,000 men take months to assemble.

A warning sign shows when the season is wrong, and every cost line opens its own gain and lose list so you can see where the number comes from.

There are no class or province settings, and a region or country levy draws only your royal provinces; lords' men come as their retinues. The screen names the bigger classes and sums the small ones as "others". A levy takes men from every class in proportion to its share of the men who can be called, and from every province of a region by its share of the region's men. Veterans come along by their share of the pool too: if Troyes has 2,000 recruits of whom 400 are veterans, a levy of 1,000 brings 200 veterans.

Each thousand gathers in 14 days (3 days once you know W18 Railway mobilization, so several can come in one week) and joins the province's garrison: troops without a commander. They man the province's castle if it has one, and wait on the province card's Troops tab (its Garrison page), listed with their arms and drill; only you see them. They can't march, fight in the field or train until a commander picks them up into his army, which happens at once. A commander standing in the province does it from the Troops tab ("La Hire takes 1,000" or "takes all") or from his own screen ("Take in 1,000" or "Take in all"); a commander waiting at court can be put at their head with "Lead". A commander can also leave men behind as a province's garrison. While they are away their job slots stay empty, so a building that falls under half filled goes fallow and pays no tax and makes no wares.

This worked example comes from the design and is drawn as a region levy; today the same levy is raised province by province. Champagne, late summer 1444. France is at war with Burgundy. Champagne has 4 royal provinces, about 260,000 people, 65,000 fighting-age men and 7,900 men who can be called, 65 % of them farmers. It is 3 weeks before harvest. A levy of 5,000 costs 100 gold and 2,500 rations, cuts the Champagne harvest by 6 %, and leaves each province 37 % of its pool. The levy takes 3,250 farmers, 550 herders, 500 homeless, 300 woodsmen, 200 craftsmen, 150 seafolk and 50 others. The screen reads "Ready in 2 weeks at Troyes" as 5,000 levies, with a harvest warning. Push the slider to 6,000 and each province keeps only 24 % of its pool, so all four get the Conscription grievance.

#The recruit pool

Each province keeps three soldier counts:

  • Recruits here: the men who can be called right now, never above the ceiling.
  • Fallen from here: men from this province killed in the last 10 years.
  • Veterans here: men home from war who were trained or stood in a battle.

Every January the pool refills by a quarter of the gap to the ceiling, or a third with a muster master as official: Troyes with a ceiling of 2,200 and 1,000 recruits gets back to 1,300 (1,400 with a muster master). It grows when disbanded men come home, and a growing population raises the ceiling. It shrinks for levies, students enrolling, famine, plague, men dying in war and the men of a rising. While a province riots, only half its recruits can be called; the ceiling stays the same, and the whole pool can be called again when the riot ends.

Conscription. If a levy leaves a province's recruits below a quarter of its ceiling, the province gets the Conscription grievance. If the province has Fallen from here, the line is a third instead. With W15 Mass conscription it is only below a tenth, fallen or not.

The fallen. Each soldier levied in a province who dies adds one to its Fallen from here, which fades by a tenth each January. For each 1 % of a province's people fallen, its births fall by 0.2 % for 10 years.

Veterans. Disbanded regiments and levies who were trained or stood in a battle come home as Veterans here. They fade by a tenth each January from old age, and also leave by dying or going off as settlers.

#What a levy costs at home

  • Births: while fighting-age men are away, births fall by the share of them away.
  • The harvest: a levy in the sowing or harvest season cuts the harvest by twice the share of farmers taken, and by 1.5 times that at harvest. In the north, sowing is in March and April and the harvest season is August and September; in the south, sowing is in September and October and the harvest season is February and March. A winter levy costs no harvest. Rice lands have two crops a year: sowing in May–June and November–December, harvest in February–March and August–September. In a pastoral province, taking herders during the spring move (March to May in the north, September to November in the south) cuts herd growth by twice the share of herders taken, and the harvest is untouched.
  • Food: men in a training camp eat 1.5 rations a month and horses 3, from the training castle's store first and then its province. A man eats 2 rations on campaign and 1 at home. Before an army marches abroad it fills a reserve of reserve size × 2 rations per man per month, from the castle store first, then the locals.

#Training

Training is ordered from the commander screen, for the levy infantry in that commander's army, while the army stands at a castle or drill yard of yours: set how many thousand with − and +, then tap the troop type, which shows its months and gold. Away from a training place the screen tells you to march there first, and training already under way holds while the army is away. The commander screen also shows the army's men by troop type, drill and break point, the commander's skills, its companies to split, merge or leave as garrison, its food reserve and food used a day and a month, and its pay.

Levies can only be trained at a training place: a castle or a drill yard of yours. Each has a training room, the number of men it trains at once:

A castle and a drill yard in the same province add their rooms together. You pick the troop type, how many men and the place; the men walk there and start. If you order training from a town with neither, the men march to the nearest place that can train. Some things speed training up or change it:

  • A Marshal as the province's official makes training there 25 % faster.
  • Veterans train in half the time, so a regiment made mostly of veterans finishes early.
  • Retraining a regiment to a newer troop type takes half the months and half the gold, and it keeps its drill and scars.
  • Until the order is done the men are still levies. If you call them out of training to fight, they go as levy infantry and the months spent are lost.
  • A castle or town under siege stops all training in it.
  • The 3 free months count from the day the levy has gathered. If training runs past them, you pay for the extra months. Pikes and light cavalry (3 months) finish inside the free months; musketeers (5 months) cost 2 months of pay and knights (9 months) 6.

When training finishes, the regiment has its troop type, drill 3 (drill 4 if trained under a Marshal), pay by its troop type and a break point of 30 % plus its troop level. Untrained levies stay at drill 0 and break at 25 %. Training never changes a regiment's kind: a trained levy regiment is still a levy. No levy, trained or not, goes home on its own: it stays until you disband it or leave it as a garrison.

Pay by troop type, per 1,000 men a month: foot 200 gold, gunners 250, horse 400, knights 600, and gun crews 1 gold per crew of 15 (10 gold for 150 men). Each troop type belongs to one of these groups:

Champagne continued. Of the 5,000 levies, 2,500 train into pikes at the castle of Troyes and 500 into light cavalry at Reims, while 2,000 march at once as levies. The pikes cost 150 gold and 25 loads of arms and eat 11,250 rations in 3 months. The light cavalry cost 50 gold and 500 horses and eat 6,750 rations. Training eats 18,000 rations from the castle stores at Troyes and Reims, and no pay is owed. Filling a 2-month reserve then takes 23,000 rations. From the 4th month the army costs 1,000 gold a month in pay and eats 11,500 rations a month on campaign, about 6,500 more than the men would eat at home.

#Standing regiments and retinues

With W12 Standing regiments you can sign on a trained regiment. It stays under arms in peace, keeps its drill and scars, and is paid 1.5 times, all year round. Its men are gone from their province's pool and their slots stay empty. Housed in a barracks in peace, a standing regiment gives the province no Soldiers quartered; without a barracks it is quartered on the people like any army in winter.

Lords raise their retinues by the same rules, with their own wealth and at their own castles. Their traits decide what they train: a Warlike lord trains knights and heavy infantry, a Greedy one keeps levies.

#What recruiting gives back

  • Taking homeless men means less theft and begging, and the province may lose the Homeless state.
  • While men serve abroad, their home province has fewer mouths to feed.
  • Each month a fifth of the pay of soldiers training, standing or quartered in one of your provinces is spent there, as trade for its market folk, and its tax owed rises by the same amount.
  • Disbanded men can go as soldier settlers to a frontier or colony province with free tiles. They clear tiles twice as fast as other settlers and become farmers, and that province needs one grievance more to riot for 10 years.
  • When an army disbands, a tenth of its loot goes to the provinces its men came from, split by how many men each gave, raising their local store and tax owed for a year.

#What recruiting takes

  • Disbanded men go back into the emptiest slots of their province; those with no free slot become homeless.
  • On disbanding, the arms always go back to the store. You are not asked.
  • An army that disbands within 3 months of a plague or high-sickness campaign brings a plague rumour home to 1 in 4 of the provinces it disbands into.
  • Students called to arms stop making research.

#Commanders from officials

Every army has a commander, and no army gets one by default. Your way to new commanders is to commission officials; your ruler and heirs may also lead, lords lead only their own retinues, and hired companies bring their own commander. A captain who rises from the ranks, an academy graduate or an exile first joins your service as an official.

Any official can be commissioned to lead an army or stand in one as a captain. They leave the province they ran and start with the skills and trait offer of their type:

General or admiral. The official's screen offers two commissions side by side: Commission as general and Commission as admiral, each showing the Might, Cunning and Logistics he would start with. A general leads only armies; an admiral leads only fleets and starts with his type's sea skills (see Naval), his years in a port province counting double. He stays a general or an admiral until released. The 1444 commanders and mercenary captains are generals, a hired fleet's captain an admiral. An AI fleet that needs an admiral and has none commissions one, from its own port's official first.

When a commander dies. Commanders age and die like everyone else (see Your Realm). When a general dies, his men stand as the garrison of the crown province they are in; in a lord's land, in enemy land or in your own land held by an enemy, they walk to the nearest crown province you hold and garrison it there. When an admiral dies, his ships sail for the nearest port of your own. On the way they take no orders and can't be caught. Zoomed out you see no banner or fleet token for them; close in, you see the men walking and the ships sailing home. A card asks whether his second in command, or his flag captain, should lead them in his place instead (see Historical Events).

At 5 years served the type's highest skill gains +1, and +1 more at 10, and no skill goes past 5. When skills tie, Logistics gains first, then Cunning, then Might, so a Marshal with 5 years served has Logistics 3. You pick the offered trait or keep the one rolled. A commander gets the realm's current style for their era and country; a Marshal may take any style your realm has seen in a battle it fought. Years served count where the official was assigned.

The costs. A commission costs 50 gold once (a provisional number, raised from 10 so commanders are not handed out freely). In the field an official earns their wage ×3 as commander or ×2 as captain. They bring a staff of 100 (commander) or 50 (captain) who eat 2 rations each a month from the army's reserve, and their horses (30 for a commander, 15 for a captain) eat 3 each. Staff eat but are not soldiers: they do not count toward foraging or the column size.

The empty province. When you commission an official you choose how their province is run:

ChoiceEffect
Leave itHalf tax, half recruits, and the province is Ungoverned
Move another official inThe moved official starts at half effect, and their old province becomes the empty one
Name a deputyFull tax and recruits, no type effect, for 1 gold a month; needs 20 clerks in the province
Ask the lord next doorA loyal neighbouring lord takes the recruits for his retinue and half the tax, grows a step more loyal, and may ask for it as a fief when the war ends

When you release the official you send them to a province of your choice. Their years served restart at half, unless they return to the province they left and a deputy ran it.

The steward of Reims. The steward of Reims has 8 years served and Reims has at least 20 clerks. Commissioned as commander with a deputy named, the steward has Might 0, Cunning 1, Logistics 4 and the trait Careful, the deputy costs 1 gold a month, and the army eats 20 % less. When the lords are called, the Proud Count of Foix refuses to bring his retinue and the Duke of Bourbon comes. After two wins the army comes home in spring with 700 of the 5,000 dead: the steward returns to Reims at full effect at once, Troyes' births dip for a decade, and 1,400 veterans return to Troyes who will train in half the time.

#The Marshal

The Marshal is an official type trained for war. You get one from an academy, by hiring one at three times a normal official's price, or by raising a captain who stood in 3 won battles (one offer per won battle). While a Marshal runs a province, training there is 25 % faster and regiments leave with drill 4. A Marshal is paid twice an official's wage in war or peace, and one who spends 10 years in a province with no war loses 1 from their best skill.

#What official commanders mean

  • They never refuse a command, never go home with their men mid-war, and never defect with land.
  • When their army wins a battle, every lord grows one step more loyal (up to Loyal; a lord in revolt stays there), the ruler's victories rise by one, and no single lord grows from it.
  • An official who returns with 3 or more victories gives 1.5 times their type's effect where they serve, for as long as they hold that post.
  • A Proud lord serving as captain under an official wavers a step each season, and refuses to bring his retinue unless the war aim is his Want.
  • An official commander brings no retinue of their own.
  • If one is killed or captured you lose a commander and an official; a ransom brings both back.
  • Any commander with 8 victories who has led the same regiments for 5 years becomes "their man". An official at 6 victories asks for land: refusing makes them Ambitious, granting turns them into a lord with their old soldiers as his retinue.

Other leaders: the ruler and heirs can lead, lords lead only their own retinues (as captains in a royal army), hired captains come with their mercenary companies, and exiles who ask to serve become officials of the type closest to their past.

#Names and ranks

Every commander is named the way his own people name someone: a Castilian is "Rodrigo Manrique", a Hungarian "Hunyadi János" and a Japanese "Hosokawa Katsumoto", family name first where the language puts it first. The great commanders of the start go by their own names too: Bertrand du Guesclin, Gazi Evrenos Bey, Vettor Pisani, Xu Da. He also carries the rank of his post in that language: a Portuguese commander leading an army is "Marechal Fernão Coutinho" and the same man at the head of a fleet is "Almirante Fernão Coutinho"; an Ottoman admiral is "Kapudan Mehmed Pasha", a Japanese general "Hosokawa Katsumoto Taishō". The rank changes with the post, so an admiral sent back to court is a general again. A mercenary company's captain is named and ranked in the language of the land the company gathers in ("Hauptmann Ulrich von Hallwyl", "Condottiero Niccolò Piccinino") and keeps that name in your service. A people with no word for a post in the game's data shows the name alone.

The ranked name is used on the army row of a province card, the commander screen, the Military tab's Commanders list, the army picker, fleet names on the Navy tab and in port, and the Mercenaries tab. Under it, the small line with his skills starts with his post in plain English (commander, admiral or hired captain), so you never need to know the word. Dispatches and short buttons use the plain name.

#The computer players

The AI uses the same levy and training commands. Its catalogue personality sets how much of its pool it dares to levy and what it trains: a militarist levies deep and trains expensive troop types, a merchant republic levies little and hires companies instead, a steppe realm trains horse archers. It avoids levying in harvest months unless an enemy army is in its land, and it commissions stewards and wardens first, keeping tax collectors in their seats. It commissions an official only when at least 1,000 of its men have no commander and no free commander can go to them, so it never keeps more commanders than its armies need.

#Each month

A turn is one week. Everything below happens once a month, at the end of the turn in which the new month begins, with the same amounts as before.

Each province is updated once a month: training months tick down and finished regiments come out, births are cut by men away and fallen, pay starts for levies past their free months, a fifth of pay is spent where soldiers are, the empty province's effects apply, and Marshal idleness counts up. Once a year, in the turn in which 1 January falls, the pool refills and Fallen and Veterans fade. A levy itself is worked out in one calculation when you tap it.

#What you see

  • The levy screen, opened from a province card: one men slider in thousands; Gold now, Food now, Pay, Harvest, Pool left and Ready in; a season and harvest warning sign; a gain and lose list behind every cost line.
  • The commander screen: the army's stats, troops, food and pay, and the training choice (troop type, thousands of men, castle or drill yard).
  • The Troops tab of the province card (its Garrison page): troops without a commander, levies still gathering and the commanders who can pick them up.
  • Where to settle men: soldier settlers for a frontier or colony province.
  • The commission choice: commander or captain, the trait to pick, and the four ways to run the empty province.
  • Province stats: Recruits here, Fallen from here and Veterans here.

#How it connects

  • Provinces: classes, job slots, homeless, officials, grievances and births; recruiting writes back empty slots, Conscription and the three soldier counts.
  • Your-Realm: gold, food and arms stores, lords and their loyalty, officials' wages, schools and academies.
  • Warfare: takes the companies and commanders recruiting makes, and reports deaths, victories, loot and sieges back.
  • Buildings: castle steps, the drill yard and its barracks step set training room.
  • Research: W12, W15 and W18 unlock signing on, raise call shares and speed gathering.
  • Troop-Movement: gathered levies and trained regiments become movers, and levies walk to their training place.
  • Naval: recruiting sailors reuses the levy with its own ceiling for seafolk (sailors at sea are not at home to be levied), and admirals reuse the commission and the empty province.
  • Rebellions: armed veterans, a rising's men drawn from the pool, and the Conscription grievance.
  • Historical-Events: plague rumours brought home and generals who grow too big.
  • Time-and-Turns: the month tick, the seasons and the order of monthly steps.
  • Government: the Ungoverned state and lords' fiefs.
  • Diplomacy: war aims (a lord's Want) and exiles asking to serve.
