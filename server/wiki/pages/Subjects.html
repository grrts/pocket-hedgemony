Subjects

Subjects are countries on the map that answer to another country, their overlord. This system decides what types of subject exist, what each side owes the other, how loyal a subject is and why, and how a subject is made, tightened, loosened, folded into your realm, released or lost. Loyalty is never a hidden number: it is one of four steps, moved each season by named reasons you can read on the subject's card.

#How it works

#The ladder of rule

All land sits on one ladder of rule: royal land (run by your officials), lord's land (run by a named lord inside your realm), subject realm, and independent. The one rule that separates a lord from a subject: a subject has its own country on the map with its own treasury, army, lords and diplomacy, while a lord is a card inside your realm. At the start, any catalogue subject that is not its own map nation becomes a lord of its overlord, his card filled from the catalogue's history.

#The subjects at the start

The relations data has eight subject types, which are mapped onto the six: vassal to Vassal; fief to Vassal with Held of the Church or Held in fief (Naples from the Pope, Andorra from Foix); appanage to Vassal with Kin; tributary to Tributary; personal union to Union; frontier commandery to March; colony to Colony; and estate (Catalonia, Flanders, the Castilian magnates, Moravia and others) to a lord of its crown, never a separate country. Event subjects map the same way, with protectorate and puppet becoming Protectorate and dominion a Colony at Self-governing. On 20 September 1378 the 228 subject records minus 24 estates give 204 subjects: 126 vassals, 68 tributaries, 4 unions, 4 marches and 2 colonies. (The 1444 checkpoint has 135.)

Raising a lord. A Loyal or Wavering lord holding at least 3 provinces can be raised to a Vassal, or to a March if his land borders a foreign realm (you choose): his provinces become his own country on the map and his retinue its army. A Wavering lord raised gets Given a crown +1 for 10 years; a Loyal one gets nothing extra. He leaves your lord capacity and counts against your subject limit; his land leaves your realm, you get a share instead of half his taxes, and he may make his own friends abroad.

#Obligations both ways

  • When anyone but you attacks a subject you owe defence, you are called as if you were its ally. Refusing gives you an Oath broken, the subject You left it alone, and every other subject −1 for 5 years.
  • Each year a March's stipend is missed, it gets −1.
  • A Union shares your ruler, so every war of that ruler is the Union's war, and its lords' peace voices add to yours.
  • Two subjects of one overlord can't fight without leave. You pick a side (the loser drops a step) or forbid it (both −1 for 5 years).

#Subject limit

The subject limit is how many subjects your ruler can watch over. It comes from Government, starting from the catalogue's administrative capacity: very low 2, low 3, moderate 5, high 7, very high 9. It is a separate number from lord capacity. Each direct subject counts by its weight (Tributary, March and Held in fief ½, the rest 1). Over the limit, every subject gets Stretched thin −1, and for each point you are over, one more of the furthest subjects takes −1 more (11 of 7 hits the four furthest).

Subjects with subjects. A subject that holds subjects counts 3 times its type's weight plus 1 for each of its own subjects, and gets Master of others −3, which goes away when it releases them. Only the direct overlord acts on a subject; a subject's own subjects pay you nothing and count nothing toward your subject limit. When a subject is folded in or falls, its subjects step up to you, keeping their loyalty. March, Colony and Protectorate can't hold subjects.

#Folding in

Folding in makes a subject's ruler one of your lords and removes its country from the map. It needs: Loyal for the last 10 years, a subject for 20 years or more, a land path or your sea route to it, room for one more lord in lord capacity, and a type other than Tributary or Self-governing colony. At the start each subject counts as held since its dataset start date (20 years when unknown), and one Loyal at the start counts as Loyal for 10 years.

You keep one envoy at its court for 1 month per province; if the subject drops to Wavering, the folding stops and the envoy comes home. When it completes, its ruler becomes one lord holding all its provinces (its own lords merged into his card), with its buildings, its army as his retinue and its treasury as his wealth. Its provinces get Foreign rule for 10 years unless it shared your faith and people.

  • An Outpost or Settled colony folded in becomes royal land at once, needing your officials.
  • Merge the crowns folds in a Union after 50 years in union with the junior crown Loyal: its provinces join the realm, its lords become yours one by one keeping their loyalty, and its laws become a charter in its provinces.
  • When the ruler of a Loyal Vassal or Union dies without an heir, you can inherit and fold it in at once, ignoring the 10 and 20 years. For other subjects its lords pick a new ruler, and you may back a candidate with an envoy.
  • With the event flag doctrine of lapse, any subject whose ruler dies without a natural heir is folded in, and every other subject takes −1 for 10 years.

#Losing subjects

  • Drifting. A Disloyal subject stops paying, refuses calls, and bargains with foreign envoys for a backer that agrees to declare Break a subject when it rises.
  • Rising. A Disloyal subject rises to In revolt when it has a backer at peace and willing, or its overlord is at war elsewhere with over half its peace voices for peace, or an event says so; the seasonal count alone never goes past Disloyal. Every other Disloyal subject of yours that borders it or shares its backer rises too. The independence war starts with its aim fixed as Independence and its backer joining. If you win, the peace can restore it one type tighter, or fold it in at once if it shares a border. If it wins, it goes free with a Hostile stance toward you.
  • Changing masters. A Disloyal subject bordering a rival of yours can ask to pass to that rival; the rival takes it when it fits within the rival's subject limit. It becomes the rival's subject of the same type, and you and the rival turn Hostile toward each other.
  • Taken by force. Break a subject is a war declared on you, the overlord; the subject answers by its type and loyalty like any call. If the attacker wins, it chooses whether the subject goes free or becomes its own.
  • The overlord's fall. If an overlord is destroyed or becomes someone's subject, its Tributaries and Marches go free, and its Vassals, Unions and Colonies go to whoever took its capital if it wants them, otherwise free.

#Making a subject

  • A peace term Become a subject (over three quarters peace voices): the winner picks Vassal or Tributary, or Protectorate from era VI (era IV for an overseas trade empire). A distant loser with no land path can only be a Tributary.
  • After a 20-year tribute term has run, the receiver can offer the payer Tributary by envoy, which a weaker payer accepts.
  • Winning a war to seat a pretender: the realm becomes your Vassal or ally, the new ruler starting Loyal.
  • By agreement through Diplomacy: when its count says yes to Offer overlordship or Ask for protection, the smaller country becomes a Tributary, or a Vassal if it asked for that.
  • A ruler inheriting another crown after a marriage makes a Union.
  • A frontier grant: give your titles and a gift to a frontier chief who is nobody's subject and shares your border, and he becomes a March.
  • Chartering a group of your colonial provinces in one distant region makes one Colony.
  • Events (vassalize, create_country … subject_of, relation: personal_union) create the type they name; a created country is a Colony at Self-governing unless named otherwise.

#France, 1444 to 1453 (on the 1444 checkpoint world)

France has high administrative capacity and a subject limit of 7, with 10 vassals and the appanage of the Dauphiné.

  • The start. Brittany starts Wavering and Armagnac Loyal. France uses 11 of its subject limit of 7, so every subject, Brittany and Foix included, has Stretched thin, and the four furthest take −1 more.
  • Folding in the Dauphiné. The Dauphiné is a Loyal Vassal with Kin, a subject since 1349, with 3 provinces. France sends one envoy for 3 months; the Dauphin becomes a great lord inside France, which now uses 10 of its limit of 7.
  • Two small vassals. Folding in the French minor lordships and Nevers over the next years brings it to 8 of 7, and lord capacity fills up instead.
  • Brittany is courted. England stations an envoy at Brittany's court; France stations a resident there and arranges a marriage. The season ends at 3 up and 3 down, so Brittany stays Wavering. When war with England resumes in 1449 and France calls, Brittany comes with half its army, late.
  • Armagnac. France sets Armagnac's tribute to heavy to pay for the war. Armagnac turns Disloyal, and an event makes it rise. France wins, the peace folds Armagnac in at once because it shares a border, and every other subject takes Force used −1 for 5 years.

#Playing as a subject

Every playable subject at the start (94 on 20 September 1378) can be played. You see your own card from below with the same badges, and can pay and be protected, stop paying, find a backer, wait for your overlord's bad war, or marry into its house to inherit its crown (a union can run either way). A Loyal subject that politely asks by envoy for a looser type gets it if the overlord's AI is cautious or over its subject limit. A subject that wins its independence war can make its old overlord its own subject in the same peace if the voices allow.

#The computer players

As overlord, an AI folds in Loyal subjects when lord capacity allows and its personality likes centralising, loosens a Disloyal subject when at war elsewhere, stations residents on its biggest Wavering subjects, and never goes more than 1 over its subject limit. As a subject, a Disloyal AI looks for a backer; an opportunist rises the moment its overlord is losing a war, a cautious one waits for a backer and its neighbours. As a rival, it courts its rivals' Wavering subjects and picks Break a subject against the overlord with the most Disloyal subjects. Loyalty is worked out once a season per subject from cached reasons.

#Each month

A turn is one week; these steps run once a month, at the end of the turn in which the new month begins. Each month tribute is paid, tighten and fold-in envoys progress (stopping if the subject wavers), and risings are checked. At each season's end reasons are recomputed and loyalty moves. Each year march stipends fall due and colony stages are checked.

#What you see

  • The subjects list (a tab on the country card): how much of the subject limit is used, monthly tribute in gold and grain, and one row per subject with its type icon, loyalty, up and down arrow counts, what it pays and its worst reason, sorted worst first.
  • The subject card, in the lord card's frame: type, ruler, provinces, army, treasury, its own subjects, loyalty with its reasons, what it pays and how it comes when called, and the action buttons.
  • World zoom: a subject in its direct overlord's colour with a 2-pixel diagonal hatch in its own colour and a dotted border with the overlord, never two hatches stacked.
  • Region zoom: the type's icon on the subject's capital, its frame tinted by loyalty (green, yellow, orange, red).
  • The subjects map mode: your subjects coloured by loyalty, with a line to each courted subject from the courting country's capital.
  • Long-press a subject on the map (or select it and press the card button on a controller) to open its card.

#Subjects in the game today

  • Where: your realm's Diplomacy tab lists your Subjects: how much of your reach they use (from your administrative capacity: 2 to 9, 5 where it is unknown), the tribute they send each month, and a row per subject with its type, loyalty, arrows up and down, what it pays and its worst reason. Tap a subject to open its card.
  • The subject's card (its own Diplomacy tab): its loyalty and every reason behind it as a badge, what it pays and how it comes to your wars, and your actions: set the tribute (None, Light, Normal, Heavy, Crushing), Send a gift (six months of its income, +1 for 5 years), Loosen to tributary (Vassal or Protectorate, +2 for 10 years) and Release (it goes free as your friend).
  • What they pay: the type's share times the tribute level, all of it while Loyal, half while Wavering and nothing while Disloyal. A Vassal pays 10 in 100 of its taxes; a Tributary 15 in 100 of its income. It shows as Tribute in your Treasury tab.
  • Loyalty is counted at the end of every season: two more arrows up than down raise it a step, two more down lower it, never twice the same way within a year. You hear when it moves. A Disloyal subject refuses your calls to arms.
  • Making subjects: send an envoy to Offer overlordship to a smaller realm (it becomes your tributary) or Ask for protection of a larger one (you become its vassal), or demand tribute. The other realms offer overlordship too.
  • Your envoys at their court: Station a resident (+1 loyalty, takes one of your envoys while he stays), Tighten to vassal (a Loyal tributary, one envoy for 6 months) and Fold into your realm (Loyal for 10 years, a subject for 20, sharing a border, and room under your lords cap; one envoy for a month per province). When folding in completes, their land is yours, held by their ruler as one of your lords, and the country leaves the map. Other realms fold in their subjects too, one at a time.
  • Rising: a Disloyal subject may rise and declare its independence war. The other realms' subjects rise when their overlord is losing a war elsewhere or a stronger enemy of it waits next door, and their Disloyal neighbours rise with them. Lose land in the peace and the subject is taken back one type tighter (its fellow subjects remember the force used); otherwise it is free. If you are a subject and Disloyal, Rise for independence is on your Diplomacy tab.
  • Their heir: Take a hostage from a Loyal or Wavering subject: his heir lives at your court (+1 loyalty) until you Send the heir home or 20 years pass.
  • Backers: a Disloyal subject with a stronger enemy of its overlord next door rises with that realm at its side; the backer declares war on the overlord at once.
  • The Subjects map mode: from the map mode menu, your realm in its colour, your subjects green, amber or red by loyalty, your overlord purple. Tap one for its Diplomacy tab.
  • On the realms map, zoomed out: every subject is washed in its direct overlord's colour with thin diagonal stripes (2 map pixels wide) of its own colour, and its border with that overlord is dotted instead of a solid line, so Brittany reads as part of France at a glance; a subject of a subject shows only its own overlord's colour, never a second set of stripes. Occupied land keeps its occupation hatch, and zoomed in each realm is drawn in its own colour again.
  • Personal unions: a marriage is a path to a crown. When a ruler dies without a son, because his heir died first or none was born, each house married into his has a 1 in 10 chance of a claim on his throne, held for 20 years. One such death in 4 leaves no heir at all, and the crown passes to the ruler of the house married most often into his, as a Union, if neither realm is at war and the inheriting realm answers to no one. Your own line is spared that: you can inherit, but are never inherited. Your Diplomacy tab's Your house lists the houses married into yours and your claims; another realm's tab says when your houses are married or you claim its throne.
  • Playing as a subject: on your own Diplomacy tab, or your overlord's card, Your overlord shows your card as they see it, with the same badges, then Your choices. Stop paying keeps your tribute but makes you Disloyal a step each season (Withholds tribute −2); Pay tribute again ends it. Ask to loosen (Loyal only) makes you a tributary when your overlord is cautious (unrest or outside threats high) or over its reach, and says when they would refuse. Find a backer names the rivals of your overlord, at peace, next door and as strong, who would join your rising; the first of them does. Their wars show how many of their voices want peace, the moment to rise. Marry into their house opens their card: if their line dies out and your house is married most into theirs, you inherit their crown and they become your union.
  • Turning the tables: in your war for independence, once you hold more provinces than your old master and over three quarters of its voices want peace, Make them your subject ends the war with them as your vassal (or tributary without a land border).
  • The subject card: framed like a lord's card, with its kind, its ruler as the start data names him (Duke John IV, age 39 · House Montfort), provinces, army, treasury and its own subjects.
  • Long-press: hold a finger on a realm on the map (for the Settings long-press time) to open its card at the Diplomacy tab.
  • Merge the crowns: a Union cannot be folded in. Once it has been in union 50 years and is Loyal, Merge the crowns on its card joins its provinces to your realm at once, its lords serving you with the loyalty they have. When the ruler of a Loyal Vassal or Union dies with no heir, its overlord takes it at once.
  • Courting: on another realm's subject, Court their loyalty stations one of your envoys at its court: it counts Courted (−1) against its overlord, and a Disloyal subject may rise. Each season there is 1 in 4 they find out (−25 with the overlord, fading over 10 years) and he comes home. Your own subjects' cards show Courted when someone courts them, and name who once you have a resident there; the Subjects lens draws a dotted line from the courting realm. Rival and hostile neighbours court your Wavering and Disloyal subjects too.
  • Events: historical events can make a subject (becomes a protectorate of …), loosen or tighten it, add or lift a loyalty reason for some months, and break the tie (released … goes free as a friend). What the event will do is written on its card before you choose, and the change lands on the subject's card and the Subjects lens like any other. Events can also ask how loyal a subject is, or how many subjects you hold.
  • Not yet: the succession war of an inherited realm's lords, and harming a hostage come later; distance gives no reason yet. Faith and language do: the same state faith or court language pulls a subject closer, a different faith pushes it away, more so where you persecute it.
